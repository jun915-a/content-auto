Datadog’s shutdown of CoScreen left users in limbo—but four original founders are reviving the concept with **Petal**, an open-source, free alternative. Discover how this tool could revolutionize remote collaboration and what it means for the future of work.

## 🔑 The Core of This Topic
Petal is the **open-source rebirth of CoScreen**, the popular remote collaboration tool that allowed teams to mirror their screens in real time. After Datadog discontinued CoScreen in late July, its original team—now independent—has reimagined it as a **fully free, community-driven** alternative. This move isn’t just a technical revival; it’s a **cultural shift** toward decentralized, user-owned tools in the era of remote work.

## ⚡ 5-Second Key Points
- **Free forever**: No subscription fees, no hidden costs—Petal is **100% open-source** under the MIT License.
- **Built by original CoScreen creators**: Four core team members from the Datadog era are leading the project.
- **Cross-platform support**: Works on **Windows, macOS, and Linux**, with plans for mobile expansion.
- **Privacy-first design**: No data collection, self-hosting options, and end-to-end encryption in development.
- **Community-driven**: Users can contribute to code, suggest features, or even host their own instances.

## 📈 Detailed Breakdown
**Petal’s Technical Foundation**
Petal inherits CoScreen’s **mirroring technology** but ditches the proprietary backend for a **modular, open architecture**. The tool lets users share their screens in real time, annotate collaboratively, and host virtual whiteboard sessions—all without relying on centralized servers. The founders emphasize **performance and reliability**, promising low-latency connections even for large teams. Under the hood, Petal uses **WebRTC** for peer-to-peer communication, reducing dependency on third-party infrastructure.

**Why Open-Source Matters Now**
> 💡 Insight: **The shutdown of CoScreen exposed a critical flaw in vendor-locked collaboration tools.** Petal addresses this by putting control back into users’ hands. In an era where **data sovereignty** and **privacy concerns** are rising, open-source alternatives like Petal could become the standard for remote work tools. This aligns with broader trends in **self-hosted software** (e.g., Nextcloud, Matrix) where users reject black-box solutions.

**From CoScreen to Petal: The Evolution**
The original CoScreen was acquired by Datadog in 2019, but its **real-time collaboration features** remained a niche but beloved tool. When Datadog announced its shutdown, the team faced a dilemma: **abandon the project or rebuild it independently**. Their choice to launch Petal as open-source signals a **commitment to the community** that once relied on CoScreen. The founders are actively seeking **backers and contributors** to sustain development, offering transparency through a public **GitHub repository** and roadmap.

## 🎯 Real-World Impact
- **For Teams**: Petal eliminates the **cost barrier** to high-quality remote collaboration, making it accessible to startups, nonprofits, and small businesses that can’t afford enterprise tools like Miro or Zoom Whiteboard.
- **For Developers**: The open nature invites **custom integrations**, plugins, or even **forked versions** tailored to specific needs (e.g., education, healthcare).
- **For Privacy Advocates**: By avoiding data collection and offering self-hosting, Petal **challenges the dominance of ad-supported or surveillance-capable tools**, pushing the industry toward ethical alternatives.

## ✨ Conclusion
Petal isn’t just a **technical clone** of CoScreen—it’s a **philosophical reboot** of how remote collaboration tools should work. In a world where **proprietary software monopolies** stifle innovation, Petal proves that **community-driven projects** can thrive. Whether you’re a former CoScreen user or simply tired of paywalled collaboration tools, this launch is a **call to action**: **support open-source alternatives** and demand transparency in the tools we rely on daily. The future of work might just be **free, fair, and open**—one screen at a time.
