# Sandia SA3000: The Mysterious 8085 CPU from a National Lab

Uncover the secrets of the Sandia National Labs SA3000, a custom 8085 CPU. Explore its unique origins and potential applications in this deep dive.

## 🔑 The Core of This Topic
The Sandia National Labs SA3000 is a fascinating variant of the ubiquitous Intel 8085 microprocessor. Developed by Sandia National Laboratories, a US Department of Energy facility, this chip likely served specialized internal purposes, possibly for research, control systems, or secure applications, making it a rare piece of computing history.

- **Unique Origin**: Developed by Sandia National Labs, not a standard commercial chip.
- **8085 Core**: Based on the popular Intel 8085 architecture.
- **Specialized Use**: Likely intended for specific, possibly classified, applications.

## 📈 Detailed Breakdown
**Sandia SA3000 Chip**
This processor is a custom fabrication based on the Intel 8085 instruction set and architecture. Its purpose was likely tied to Sandia's research and development in areas such as nuclear weapons surety, advanced materials, or complex simulation systems where standard commercial parts might not have met stringent requirements.

**8085 Architecture**
The SA3000 leverages the well-established 8085's 8-bit data bus, 16-bit address bus, and its robust set of instructions. This allowed Sandia to utilize existing software knowledge while developing hardware tailored to their unique needs, potentially with enhanced security features or environmental hardening.
