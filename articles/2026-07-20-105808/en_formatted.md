## 🔑 The Core of This Topic
Power companies may exercise their right of way (ROW) to take land for data center infrastructure, but this comes with significant restrictions and requirements. The key is understanding when and how this can happen.

## ⚡ 5-Second Key Points
- **Point 1**: Power companies can take land for ROW with landowner consent.
- **Point 2**: ROW can be used for data center infrastructure such as transmission lines and substations.
- **Point 3**: Landowners have the right to negotiate compensation for land taken.

## 📈 Detailed Breakdown
**Eminent Domain**
When a power company needs land for data center infrastructure, they may use eminent domain to take the land. Eminent domain is the power of the government to take private property for public use, provided they pay just compensation. Landowners have the right to negotiate this compensation.

**Right of Way**
A power company may also exercise their ROW to take land for data center infrastructure. ROW is a strip of land along a utility corridor where a power company can build and maintain their infrastructure. Landowners can grant consent for the power company to use their land for ROW.

> 💡 Insight: Landowners have significant rights when dealing with power company land grabs, including the right to negotiate compensation and consent to ROW.

## 🎯 Real-World Impact
- Data center infrastructure can be built on private land with proper compensation to landowners.
- Power companies must follow strict guidelines when taking land for data center infrastructure.
- Landowners can protect their rights by understanding their options and negotiating with power companies.

## ✨ Conclusion
When dealing with power company land grabs, it's essential to understand the rights and restrictions involved. Landowners have significant rights, including the right to negotiate compensation and consent to ROW. With the right knowledge and negotiation, landowners can protect their interests and ensure they receive fair compensation for their land.
