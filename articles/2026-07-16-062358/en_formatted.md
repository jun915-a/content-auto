Its shape, size, color contrast, and even its **sound feedback** (for screen readers) must account for users who navigate via keyboard, voice, or assistive tech. Without considering these layers, you’re designing for **one user out of billions**. Start by asking: *What if my user can’t see, hear, or move as I do?* That question alone forces you to **rethink every detail**—from hover states to focus indicators.\n\n**Element 2: The Invisible Infrastructure**\n> 💡 Insight: **Accessibility isn’t an add-on; it’s the scaffolding.**\n\nBehind every button lies a **web of dependencies**: ARIA labels, semantic HTML, keyboard navigation paths, and fallback mechanisms for when technology fails. For example, a button labeled ‘Submit’ must also have a **clear, concise ARIA role** (e.g., `button`) and a **visible focus state** for keyboard users. Skipping these steps is like building a skyscraper without an elevator—**exclusionary by default**.\n\nThe universe here refers to the **entire ecosystem** of tools, standards (WCAG, ADA), and user behaviors you must account for. A colorblind designer might miss a red/green contrast issue, but a user with protanopia will **instantly** highlight the problem.
