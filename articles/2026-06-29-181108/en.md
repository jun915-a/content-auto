# EU ISPs Demand Accountability for Overblocking

European internet service providers (ISPs) are pushing for rightsholders to be held responsible for the damage caused by overblocking, a practice that can affect legitimate users.

## 🔑 The Core of This Topic
European ISPs are calling for rightsholders to be held accountable for the damage caused by overblocking, which can lead to legitimate users being cut off from the internet.

## ⚡ 5-Second Key Points
* **Point 1**: Overblocking affects not only infringing users but also legitimate ones.
* **Point 2**: ISPs want rightsholders to take responsibility for their actions.
* **Point 3**: Overblocking can have significant financial and reputational consequences.

## 📈 Detailed Breakdown
**Element 1**
Overblocking is a practice where internet service providers block access to certain websites or services in an effort to prevent copyright infringement. However, this practice can have unintended consequences, such as affecting legitimate users who rely on these services. ISPs are now pushing for rightsholders to take responsibility for the damage caused by overblocking.

**Element 2**
ISPs argue that rightsholders should be held accountable for the financial and reputational damage caused by overblocking. They point out that rightsholders have the means to prevent overblocking by working with ISPs to identify and block only infringing content.

> 💡 Insight: The current system places the burden of overblocking on ISPs, which can lead to a culture of fear and over-compliance.

## 🎯 Real-World Impact
- Legitimate users are affected by overblocking, which can impact their livelihoods and daily lives.
- Overblocking can lead to financial losses for ISPs, which can be passed on to consumers.
- The reputational damage caused by overblocking can be significant and long-lasting.

## ✨ Conclusion
ISPs are right to demand accountability from rightsholders for the damage caused by overblocking. It's time for rightsholders to take responsibility for their actions and work with ISPs to prevent overblocking.
