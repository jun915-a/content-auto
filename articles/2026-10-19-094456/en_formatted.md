## 🔑 The Core of This Topic
The Bouba/Kiki effect is a phenomenon where people tend to associate the non-word "bouba" with rounded shapes and "kiki" with spiky shapes. This study demonstrates that dogs also exhibit this cross-modal correspondence, linking specific sound characteristics to visual forms, suggesting a shared, fundamental cognitive process between humans and canines.

## ⚡ 5-Second Key Points
- **Shared Perception**: Dogs, like humans, link sounds to shapes.
- **Bouba/Kiki Effect**: Canines associate 'bouba' with roundness and 'kiki' with spikiness.
- **Cognitive Insight**: Reveals a common, deep-seated sensory processing mechanism.

## 📈 Detailed Breakdown
**Element 1**
Researchers presented dogs with pairs of 3D objects, one round and one spiky, while simultaneously playing either 'bouba' or 'kiki' sounds. The dogs' gaze and interaction with the objects were meticulously observed to determine if they showed a preference for the congruent shape, indicating an understanding of the sound-shape correspondence.

**Element 2**
The results showed that dogs spent significantly more time looking at the round object when hearing 'bouba' and at the spiky object when hearing 'kiki'. This strong preference confirms that dogs process these non-words and shapes in a manner consistent with the human Bouba/Kiki effect, suggesting a shared perceptual ability.

> 💡 Insight: This study challenges the notion that such abstract cross-modal mapping is unique to humans, highlighting a surprising depth in canine cognition and sensory integration.
