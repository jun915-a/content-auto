# Transatlantic Telegraph Cable Failure

The first transatlantic telegraph cable, completed in 1858, failed to connect the world due to a series of unfortunate events and design flaws.

## 🔑 The Core of This Topic
The first transatlantic telegraph cable was a remarkable engineering feat that aimed to connect the world by transmitting messages across the Atlantic Ocean. Conceived by Charles Bright and executed by the Atlantic Telegraph Company, this ambitious project seemed to defy the odds, but ultimately ended in disappointment.

## ⚡ 5-Second Key Points
* **Point 1**: The cable was laid in 1857 and took 17 days to complete.
* **Point 2**: The cable's construction and design were plagued by issues, including a faulty compressor.
* **Point 3**: The cable's failure was attributed to a combination of factors, including damage during the laying process and inadequate maintenance.

## 📈 Detailed Breakdown
**Element 1**
The initial failure of the cable occurred after just one week of operation, when it was severed by a fishing vessel. Despite repairs, the cable continued to malfunction, and its signals grew weaker over time.

**Element 2**
The design flaws of the cable, including a faulty compressor, contributed to its failure. The compressor was intended to prevent water ingress, but it ended up causing more harm than good.
