# Bug Blindness: Why We Miss Obvious Software Flaws

Discover 'bug blindness,' the psychological phenomenon that makes even experienced developers overlook glaring errors in code. Learn why it happens and how to combat it.

## 🔑 The Core of This Topic
Bug blindness is the tendency for individuals, especially those deeply familiar with a system, to overlook obvious errors or flaws within it. This cognitive bias stems from over-familiarity, making the incorrect appear normal.

## ⚡ 5-Second Key Points
- **Cognitive Tunnel Vision**: Familiarity makes us ignore what's right in front of us.
- **Assumption of Correctness**: We trust our own or others' code too much.
- **Limited Perspective**: We often review code from our own viewpoint, missing others'.

## 📈 Detailed Breakdown
**Over-Familiarity**
When you've worked on code for a long time, certain patterns become ingrained. Your brain stops actively processing them, leading you to skim over mistakes that a fresh pair of eyes would spot immediately.

**Confirmation Bias**
We tend to look for evidence that confirms our existing beliefs. If you believe a piece of code works, you'll unconsciously filter out information suggesting it doesn't.

> 💡 Insight: The more you know about something, the harder it can be to see its flaws.

**Context Switching**
Constantly jumping between different tasks and codebases fragments attention, making it harder to maintain the deep focus needed to catch subtle bugs.

## 🎯 Real-World Impact
- Critical security vulnerabilities being missed during code reviews.
- Performance issues going unnoticed in production systems.
- User-facing bugs slipping through testing phases.

## ✨ Conclusion
Combating bug blindness requires conscious effort, fresh perspectives, and structured review processes. Actively seeking out errors, rather than assuming correctness, is key to building more robust software.
