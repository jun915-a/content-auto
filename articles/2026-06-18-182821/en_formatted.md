## 🔑 The Core of This Topic
Repurposing drugs involves reusing existing medications for new medical conditions or diseases, significantly reducing development costs and time frames. This approach enables researchers to identify effective treatments for various conditions without the need for extensive clinical trials.

## ⚡ 5-Second Key Points
- **Point 1**: Saves up to 90% of the cost associated with developing new medications.
- **Point 2**: Accelerates the discovery of treatments for rare and complex diseases.
- **Point 3**: Offers a more cost-effective and efficient alternative to traditional drug development.

## 📈 Detailed Breakdown
**Element 1**
By leveraging existing medications, researchers can fast-track the identification of effective treatments, which can be particularly beneficial for patients with rare or complex conditions. This approach also reduces the risk of unforeseen side effects and adverse reactions associated with new medications.

**Element 2**
Repurposing drugs requires a multidisciplinary effort involving researchers, clinicians, and industry experts working together to identify potential uses for existing medications. This collaborative approach enables the development of innovative treatments that can make a significant impact on patient care.

> 💡 Insight: Repurposing drugs is a game-changer in the field of medicine, offering a cost-effective and efficient way to develop new treatments for various conditions.

## 🎯 Real-World Impact
- A study at King's College London found that repurposing drugs can save up to 90% of the cost associated with developing new medications.
- This approach has led to the development of new treatments for conditions such as cancer, Alzheimer's disease, and Parkinson's disease.
- Repurposing drugs has the potential to transform patient care by providing access to effective treatments for a range of conditions.
