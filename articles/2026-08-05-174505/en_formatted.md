Why Silicon Valley's assistive tech is often more barrier than bridge

## 🔑 The Core of This Topic
Silicon Valley's assistive tech, also known as the 'Disability Dongle', is meant to help people with disabilities. However, it often ends up creating more barriers than bridges. This is because many assistive technologies are designed with a narrow focus on individual impairments, ignoring the complexities of human experience.

## ⚡ 5-Second Key Points
- **Point 1**: Assistive tech often prioritizes functionality over usability.
- **Point 2**: The Disability Dongle can lead to 'technology poverty' for those who cannot afford it.
- **Point 3**: Assistive tech can perpetuate ableism and stigma.

## 📈 Detailed Breakdown
**The Problem with Functionality-First Design**
When assistive tech prioritizes functionality over usability, it can create a barrier for users who need to adapt to new technologies. This is especially true for people with cognitive or learning disabilities, who may struggle to navigate complex interfaces.

**The Cost of the Disability Dongle**
The cost of assistive tech can be prohibitively expensive, making it inaccessible to those who need it most. This can lead to a phenomenon known as 'technology poverty', where people are unable to participate in the digital economy due to lack of access to assistive technologies.

> 💡 Insight: Assistive tech should be designed with usability and accessibility in mind, rather than simply adding a 'disability feature' as an afterthought.

## 🎯 Real-World Impact
- The Disability Dongle can exacerbate social isolation and loneliness for people with disabilities.
- Assistive tech can perpetuate systemic ableism and stigma, perpetuating negative attitudes towards people with disabilities.
- The lack of accessible technology can limit economic opportunities and social mobility for people with disabilities.

## ✨ Conclusion
It's time to rethink the way we design assistive tech. We need to prioritize usability and accessibility, and recognize that assistive technologies are not just 'add-ons' but an integral part of the digital experience. By doing so, we can create a more inclusive and equitable digital landscape that benefits everyone, regardless of ability.
