## 🔑 The Core of This Topic
The European Space Agency's (ESA) Rosetta mission marked a historic milestone in space exploration with Philae's landing on Comet 67P/Churyumov-Gerasimenko in 2014. This extraordinary event is now relived in a captivating experience, taking us back to the moment when humanity first set foot on a comet.

## ⚡ 5-Second Key Points
- **Point 1**: Philae's landing marked the first time a spacecraft had touched down on a comet.
- **Point 2**: The mission provided unprecedented insights into the composition and structure of comets.
- **Point 3**: Philae's instruments revealed the comet's surface and subsurface properties.

## 📈 Detailed Breakdown
**Element 1**
The Rosetta spacecraft, launched in 2004, spent 10 years traveling to Comet 67P/Churyumov-Gerasimenko. Philae, the lander, separated from the mothership on November 12, 2014, and landed on the comet's surface on November 12, 2014.

**Element 2**
Philae's instruments collected valuable data on the comet's composition, including water, organic molecules, and minerals. The data helped scientists understand the formation and evolution of our solar system.

> 💡 Insight: Philae's landing proved that comets are not just icy bodies but also complex, dynamic systems that can provide valuable insights into the early history of the solar system.
