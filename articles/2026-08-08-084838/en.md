# Physicist's Clever Hamster Wheel Hack Uploads Runs to Strava

A physicist rigged his pet hamster's wheel to upload runs to Strava, raising questions about what constitutes 'running'.

## 🔑 The Core of This Topic
A physicist has created a device that allows his pet hamster to upload its runs to Strava, the popular running app. This innovative hack has sparked debate about what it means to be a 'runner' and whether a hamster's wheel activity should be considered running.

## ⚡ 5-Second Key Points
* **Point 1**: A physicist rigged his hamster's wheel to upload to Strava.
* **Point 2**: The hack raises questions about what constitutes 'running'.
* **Point 3**: A hamster's wheel activity may not be the same as human running.

## 📈 Detailed Breakdown
**Hamster Wheel Hacks**
The physicist's device attaches to the hamster's wheel and uses sensors to track the hamster's activity. The data is then uploaded to Strava, where it is displayed as a run.

**Strava's Response**
Strava has stated that they do not have a strict definition of 'running' and will not be removing the hamster's runs from the app. However, they do note that a hamster's wheel activity is not the same as human running.

> 💡 Insight: The hack highlights the blurred lines between what we consider 'running' and what we consider 'cute'.

## 🎯 Real-World Impact
- The hack has sparked debate about the definition of 'running'.
- It has raised questions about the validity of tracking and comparing activity data.
- It has encouraged others to think creatively about how they track and share their fitness data.

## ✨ Conclusion
The physicist's hamster wheel hack may have been a clever idea, but it has also raised important questions about what it means to be a 'runner'. As we continue to track and share our fitness data, we must consider what we are actually measuring and what it means to us.
