## 🔑 The Core of This Topic
Researchers have pinpointed the culprit behind the 60-second fluctuations in power grids: the interaction between solar wind and the Earth's magnetosphere. Specifically, it's the way energy is transferred during magnetic substorms that causes these regular oscillations.

## ⚡ 5-Second Key Points
- **Solar Wind Interaction**: The primary driver identified is the dynamic interplay between solar wind and Earth's magnetic field.
- **Magnetospheric Resonance**: Energy transfer during magnetic substorms creates specific resonant frequencies.
- **Grid Sensitivity**: Power grids, particularly their control systems, are sensitive to these natural oscillations.

## 📈 Detailed Breakdown
**Solar Wind Dynamics**
The constant stream of charged particles from the Sun, known as solar wind, carries its own magnetic field. When this field interacts with Earth's magnetic field, it can cause disturbances.

**Magnetospheric Substorms**
During periods of intense solar activity, these interactions can trigger magnetospheric substorms. These events release vast amounts of energy into the Earth's magnetosphere, creating waves.

> 💡 Insight: The energy released during these substorms isn't random; it seems to excite specific resonant frequencies within the magnetosphere that align with the observed 60-second grid fluctuations.

**Power Grid Response**
Power grids, designed to maintain stable frequency, can inadvertently amplify these natural oscillations. Their control mechanisms, intended for stability, can become susceptible to these external, periodic forces.
