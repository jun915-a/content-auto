## 🔑 The Core of This Topic
The choice between 'a' and 'an' in English depends on the **sound** of the word that follows, not the letter. 'A' is used before consonant sounds, and 'an' is used before vowel sounds. This simple rule is crucial for clear and correct English pronunciation and writing.

## ⚡ 5-Second Key Points
- **Sound, Not Letter**: Always consider the initial sound of the next word.
- **Consonant Sound**: Use 'a' before words starting with a consonant sound.
- **Vowel Sound**: Use 'an' before words starting with a vowel sound.

## 📈 Detailed Breakdown
**The Rule of Sound**
The primary factor is phonetics. If the first sound of the following word is a vowel sound (like /a/, /e/, /i/, /o/, /u/), use 'an'. If it's a consonant sound, use 'a'. This applies even if the word starts with a vowel letter or a silent consonant.

**Common Pitfalls**
Words like 'hour' (starts with 'h' but sounds like /aʊər/) correctly take 'an'. Similarly, 'university' (starts with 'u' but sounds like /juːnɪˈvɜːsəti/) takes 'a' because the initial sound is a 'y' consonant sound. Be mindful of acronyms and initialisms as well.

> 💡 Insight: Mastering 'a' vs. 'an' relies on listening carefully to the beginning sound of words, not just their spelling.
