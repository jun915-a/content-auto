# Ex-NSA Chief: Water Systems Don't Belong on the Internet

A former NSA director issues a stark warning: critical water system controllers must be isolated from the internet. This comes amid rising concerns and suspected cyberattacks targeting vital infrastructure, highlighting a severe national security risk.

## 🔑 The Core of This Topic
Former NSA chief General Keith Alexander emphatically states that water system controllers should be disconnected from the internet. This urgent call stems from the increasing threat of cyberattacks, particularly after suspected Iranian incursions on critical infrastructure. The convenience of remote access is deemed insignificant compared to the catastrophic risks of malicious control over essential public utilities.

## ⚡ 5-Second Key Points
- **Cyber Threat**: Water systems connected to the internet are highly vulnerable to attack.
- **Ex-NSA Warning**: General Alexander advocates for air-gapping critical utility controls.
- **Recent Attacks**: Suspected state-sponsored incidents underscore the immediate danger.

## 📈 Detailed Breakdown
**Element 1**
Connecting Supervisory Control and Data Acquisition (SCADA) and Industrial Control Systems (ICS) for water utilities to the public internet creates a massive attack surface. These systems, often built without robust security in mind, become easy targets for state-sponsored actors or criminal groups seeking to disrupt services or cause harm.

**Element 2**
The push for remote monitoring and control, while offering operational efficiencies, introduces unacceptable risks. Experts argue that the benefits are outweighed by the potential for contamination, service outages, or even physical damage, underscoring a fundamental conflict between convenience and critical security.

> 💡 Insight: The perceived efficiency of internet-connected industrial controls pales in comparison to the potential for widespread public health and safety crises if compromised.

## 🎯 Real-World Impact
- **Public Health Risk**: Malicious manipulation could lead to contamination of drinking water, posing severe health threats.
- **Service Disruption**: Cyberattacks can halt water supply, impacting homes, hospitals, and businesses.
- **National Security Implications**: Compromised utilities can destabilize regions and become tools in geopolitical conflicts.

## ✨ Conclusion
The consensus from cybersecurity leaders is clear: isolating critical water infrastructure from the internet is no longer optional but an imperative to safeguard public welfare and national security.
