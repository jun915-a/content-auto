Unlike traditional news consumption, it’s driven by **emotional addiction**—a dopamine-fueled loop that leaves us exhausted, cynical, and disconnected from reality. The problem isn’t just the content itself; it’s the **never-ending scroll** that hijacks our attention, warps our perception of the world, and erodes our emotional resilience. Every alarming headline or viral post triggers a cascade of stress responses, even if the content is passive. Studies show that prolonged exposure to negative stimuli **shrinks the prefrontal cortex**—the part of the brain responsible for decision-making and emotional regulation. Over time, this rewires your brain to default to **negativity bias**, where even neutral events are interpreted through a pessimistic lens. It’s not just about feeling anxious; it’s about **losing the ability to think clearly** under pressure.\n\n**Element 2**\nSocial media algorithms **exploit this vulnerability** by prioritizing content that maximizes engagement—regardless of whether it’s accurate or healthy. The result? A **feedback loop of outrage**, where outrage fuels more outrage, and outrage becomes the primary currency of online interaction. On a societal level, it **normalizes despair**, making it harder to mobilize for positive change.
