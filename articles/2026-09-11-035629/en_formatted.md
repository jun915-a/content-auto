## 📈 Detailed Breakdown
**Pilot Actions and Decision-Making**
The NTSB’s investigation reveals that the crew of Flight 1288 **initially attempted a go-around** after encountering challenges during landing, likely due to **crosswinds or runway surface irregularities**. However, the go-around was **aborted prematurely**, leading to a **hard landing** and a subsequent **runway excursion**. The pilots’ **delay in executing the go-around** and their **failure to maintain proper airspeed** during the maneuver were critical factors. 

> 💡 **Insight**: This case underscores the **high stakes of split-second decisions** in aviation, where even minor miscalculations can have catastrophic consequences. The NTSB emphasizes the need for **enhanced training in emergency procedures**, particularly for go-around scenarios.

**Runway Conditions and Technical Factors**
The runway at MIA was **not in pristine condition** at the time of the accident. Investigators found **minor surface damage** from prior landings, which may have **reduced friction** and contributed to the aircraft’s loss of directional control. Additionally, the **lack of real-time runway condition monitoring systems** on the aircraft meant pilots had **no automated alerts** about potential hazards. This gap highlights a broader industry issue where **human judgment alone** is relied upon for critical decisions.

**Regulatory and Safety Protocol Gaps**
The NTSB report criticizes **training deficiencies** in handling runway excursions, noting that **pilot response times** were slower than optimal. The investigation also points to **inconsistent runway condition reporting** between airlines and airport authorities, which could have **delayed critical awareness** of potential risks. The NTSB recommends **strengthening regulatory oversight** to ensure airlines adhere to **standardized emergency protocols**.
