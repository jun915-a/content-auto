François Englert, a Belgian physicist, was awarded the Nobel Prize in Physics in 2013 for his work on the Higgs mechanism. His contributions to the Standard Model of particle physics have left a lasting impact on the field.

## 🔑 The Core of This Topic
François Englert's groundbreaking work on the Higgs mechanism led to a deeper understanding of the universe and earned him the Nobel Prize in Physics in 2013. His research, conducted in collaboration with Peter Higgs, introduced the concept of a scalar field that permeates the universe, giving mass to fundamental particles.

## 📈 Detailed Breakdown
**The Higgs Mechanism**
Englert's work on the Higgs mechanism proposed that a scalar field, known as the Higgs field, is responsible for giving mass to fundamental particles. This concept explained why some particles have mass while others do not.

**Standard Model Contributions**
Englert's research, conducted in the 1960s, helped shape the Standard Model of particle physics. His contributions to the model provided a framework for understanding the behavior of fundamental particles and forces.

> 💡 Insight: The Higgs mechanism is a fundamental concept in particle physics, explaining how particles acquire mass.
