# Valve’s Timur Kristóf Revives Old AMD GPUs on Linux

Timur Kristóf’s groundbreaking work at XDC 2026 reveals how Valve is breathing new life into aging AMD GPUs under Linux, unlocking performance and compatibility for legacy hardware. A must-read for gamers and developers alike.

## 🔑 The Core of This Topic
Timur Kristóf’s presentation at **XDC 2026** dives into Valve’s efforts to **supercharge older AMD GPUs** on Linux, addressing long-standing compatibility issues. By leveraging kernel-level optimizations and driver enhancements, Valve aims to **extend the lifespan of hardware** like the **Radeon HD 7000 series**, making them viable for modern workloads—from gaming to productivity.

## 📈 Detailed Breakdown
**Open-Source Collaboration**
Kristóf highlights Valve’s **active contributions** to the Linux kernel and Mesa project, ensuring AMDGPU drivers evolve alongside proprietary solutions. By **bridging gaps** in Vulkan and OpenGL support, Valve’s patches allow older GPUs to handle **ray tracing and upscaling**—features once deemed impossible on hardware from a decade ago. The collaboration ensures **no vendor lock-in**, empowering users to run cutting-edge titles on budget hardware.

**Performance Gains**
> 💡 Insight: **Power efficiency** saw a **30% boost** in some tests, reducing heat output while maintaining frame rates. This is critical for **laptops and low-power setups** where thermal throttling was a major hurdle.

The breakthrough comes from **fine-tuning the GPU scheduler** and **optimizing memory bandwidth**, allowing older GPUs to **compete with mid-range cards** in less demanding scenarios. Benchmarks show **consistent 60+ FPS** in titles like *CS:GO* and *DOOM Eternal* on a **HD 7970**, a card now considered obsolete.

**Challenges & Trade-offs**
While the progress is **dramatic**, Kristóf acknowledges **limitations**. Not all games benefit equally—**AAA titles with heavy compute loads** still struggle, but **indie and retro games** now run flawlessly. Users must **enable experimental kernel flags** for best results, which may not suit all distributions. The focus remains on **stability over raw performance**, ensuring these patches don’t introduce crashes or artifacts.
