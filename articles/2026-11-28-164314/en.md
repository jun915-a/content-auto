# WebRTC Skimmers Hijack Shopping Carts on Popular Sites

Cybercriminals are exploiting WebRTC to intercept credit card details on major e-commerce platforms. This new tactic bypasses traditional security, putting shoppers' financial data at risk during peak shopping seasons.

## 🔑 The Core of This Topic
Cybercriminals are leveraging WebRTC, a technology designed for real-time communication, to inject malicious scripts into popular shopping websites. These scripts steal credit card information entered by users during checkout, bypassing standard security measures and directly transmitting sensitive data to attackers.

## ⚡ 5-Second Key Points
- **WebRTC Exploitation**: Attackers use WebRTC for real-time data exfiltration.
- **Script Injection**: Malicious code is embedded within legitimate website traffic.
- **High-Value Target**: Credit card details are harvested directly from checkout pages.

## 📈 Detailed Breakdown
**Malicious Script Injection**
Attackers find ways to inject JavaScript code, often through compromised advertising networks or third-party scripts, onto e-commerce sites. This code remains dormant until a user reaches the payment page.

**WebRTC Data Exfiltration**
Once activated, the script utilizes WebRTC's peer-to-peer capabilities to send stolen payment data directly to a server controlled by the attacker, often without triggering typical network security alerts.

> 💡 Insight: The peer-to-peer nature of WebRTC makes it harder to detect as traffic doesn't always route through traditional firewalls.

## 🎯 Real-World Impact
- Financial loss for consumers due to fraudulent transactions.
- Compromise of sensitive personal and financial data.
- Erosion of trust in online shopping platforms, especially during critical sales periods.

## ✨ Conclusion
Stay vigilant when shopping online. Be aware of unusual website behavior and ensure your browser and security software are up-to-date. Report suspicious activity immediately to protect yourself and others.
