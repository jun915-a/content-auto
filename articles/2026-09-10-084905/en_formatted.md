## 🔑 The Core of This Topic
Visa and Mastercard are not banks; they are card networks. They act as intermediaries, connecting your bank (the issuer) with the merchant's bank (the acquirer) to process your credit or debit card payments securely and efficiently.

## ⚡ 5-Second Key Points
- **Facilitate Transactions**: They enable payments between consumers, merchants, and banks.
- **Set Standards**: They define the rules and security protocols for card usage.
- **Global Reach**: They provide a universal payment infrastructure.

## 📈 Detailed Breakdown
**The Card Network's Role**
Card networks like Visa and Mastercard don't issue cards or lend money. Their primary function is to build and manage the infrastructure that allows a transaction to occur, ensuring data is transmitted securely and funds are routed correctly.

**Interchange Fees**
When you use a card, a small fee (interchange fee) is charged by the card network and passed to the merchant's bank. This fee helps cover the costs of processing the transaction and managing fraud risks.

> 💡 Insight: These networks profit from transaction volume and fees, not directly from consumer interest or merchant sales.

**Security and Innovation**
They invest heavily in security technologies like EMV chips and tokenization to protect against fraud, while also driving innovation in payment methods and expanding access to financial services globally.

## 🎯 Real-World Impact
- Enable seamless international commerce and travel payments.
- Drive innovation in digital wallets and contactless payment solutions.
- Provide essential financial tools for billions of people worldwide.

## ✨ Conclusion
Visa and Mastercard are indispensable pillars of modern commerce, quietly powering the vast majority of card-based transactions and fostering a connected global marketplace.
