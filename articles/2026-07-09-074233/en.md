## 🔑 The Core of This Topic
Unicode's transliteration rules are a set of guidelines that define how to convert characters from one script to another. However, these rules are more than just a simple conversion tool - they are Turing-complete, meaning they can perform any computation.

* **Point 2**: This has significant implications for natural language processing, machine translation, and other areas of computer science.
* **Point 3**: The discovery of Turing-completeness in transliteration rules was made by Markus Scherer in 2015.

**Element 1**
The key to understanding why transliteration rules are Turing-complete lies in their recursive nature. By breaking down complex scripts into smaller components, transliteration rules can be applied in a recursive manner to produce the desired output.

**Element 2**
This recursive approach allows transliteration rules to emulate the behavior of a Turing machine, which is a theoretical model for computation that can solve any problem. By manipulating characters and applying rules in a controlled manner, transliteration rules can perform any computation that a Turing machine can.
