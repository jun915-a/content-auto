## 🔑 The Core of This Topic
Dostoyevsky's novels are often perceived as complex and challenging due to their philosophical and psychological depth, but they're actually more relatable and engaging than you might expect. His works explore universal themes such as redemption, morality, and the human condition, making them accessible to readers from all walks of life.

- **Point 2**: His works are a critique of societal norms and the class system of his time.
- **Point 3**: Dostoyevsky's writing style is often characterized by vivid descriptions and philosophical debates.

## 📈 Detailed Breakdown
**The Power of Human Nature**
Dostoyevsky's novels are a testament to the power of human nature, highlighting our capacity for both good and evil. His characters are multidimensional and complex, making it easy for readers to relate to them on a personal level. This relatability is a key factor in making his works more accessible and engaging.

**Exploring the Human Condition**
Dostoyevsky's novels often explore the human condition, delving into themes such as morality, redemption, and the search for meaning. These universal themes are timeless and continue to resonate with readers today, making his works a valuable part of literary history.
