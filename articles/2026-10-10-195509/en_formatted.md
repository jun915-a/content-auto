## 🔑 The Core of This Topic
A **passive RF-based AI system** developed by researchers at UMass Amherst and the University of Texas at Austin can **identify hidden spy cameras**—even those disguised as ordinary objects—by analyzing **ambient RF signals** they emit. Unlike traditional camera detection methods that rely on visual or thermal imaging, this approach exploits the **radio frequency leakage** from cameras' internal components (e.g., Wi-Fi modules, sensors) to pinpoint their locations **without direct line-of-sight**, even through walls or obstacles. The system achieves **95% accuracy** in lab tests and holds promise for large-scale deployments, such as airports, government buildings, or private residences.

## ⚡ 5-Second Key Points
- **Passive detection**: No active scanning—cameras are found by **listening** to their RF emissions.
- **Long-range capability**: Works from **up to 100 meters away** in open spaces (and shorter distances indoors).
- **AI-powered**: Uses **machine learning** to distinguish camera RF signatures from background noise.
- **Disguise-proof**: Detects **hidden or disguised cameras**, including those embedded in everyday objects.
- **Privacy shift**: Could **deter unauthorized surveillance** by exposing covert systems.
