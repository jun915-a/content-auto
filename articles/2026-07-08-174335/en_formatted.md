## 🔑 The Core of This Topic
Canada's only watchmaking school is marking an impressive 80 years of operation, producing skilled horologists and keeping timekeeping traditions alive. Established in 1943, the Canadian National Institute for the Blind's (CNIB) watchmaking school in Montreal has a long history of teaching the intricacies of watchmaking to students from across the country.

## 📈 Detailed Breakdown
**The School's History**
The CNIB watchmaking school was established in 1943 as a way to provide employment opportunities for the visually impaired. Over the years, the school has adapted to meet the changing needs of the industry, incorporating new technologies and teaching methods while maintaining its commitment to preserving traditional watchmaking skills.

**Modernizing the Craft**
Today, the school offers a comprehensive program in watchmaking, covering topics such as timepiece repair, maintenance, and assembly. Students have access to state-of-the-art equipment and facilities, allowing them to gain hands-on experience and develop the skills needed to succeed in the industry.
