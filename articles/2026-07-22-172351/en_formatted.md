## 🔑 The Core of This Topic
Passkeys are a new authentication method designed to replace passwords, but it seems that the engineers behind this innovation had no understanding of how consumers think. The result is a system that may cause more problems than it solves.

## ⚡ 5-Second Key Points
* **Point 1**: Passkeys are a biometric authentication method that uses public key cryptography.
* **Point 2**: They require a physical device like a phone or a smart card to work.
* **Point 3**: Passkeys are designed to be more secure than passwords, but they may be less convenient for consumers.

## 📈 Detailed Breakdown
**Element 1**
The idea behind Passkeys is to use public key cryptography to create a secure authentication method. This is done by generating a pair of keys, one public and one private, and storing them on a physical device like a phone or a smart card. When a user wants to log in to a website or an application, they use the public key to authenticate themselves.

**Element 2**
The problem with Passkeys is that they require a physical device to work, which can be inconvenient for consumers. For example, if a user loses their phone or their smart card, they may not be able to log in to their account anymore. This can be a major problem for people who rely on their online accounts for their daily lives.

> 💡 Insight: The designers of Passkeys seem to have prioritized security over convenience, which may not be the best approach for consumers.

## 🎯 Real-World Impact
* - This may cause more problems than it solves, especially for consumers who are not tech-savvy.
* - It may lead to a decrease in online security as people start to use weaker authentication methods.
* - It may also lead to a decrease in online adoption as people become frustrated with the inconvenience of Passkeys.

## ✨ Conclusion
Passkeys are a design disaster for consumers. They prioritize security over convenience, which may not be the best approach for consumers. As a result, they may cause more problems than they solve, leading to a decrease in online security and adoption.
