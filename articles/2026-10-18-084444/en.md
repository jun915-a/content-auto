# NY Game Ownership Bill: You Could Own, Not Just License, Games

New York proposes a landmark bill shifting game ownership from licensing to true consumer ownership, demanding publishers maintain game functionality post-support.

## 🔑 The Core of This Topic
This groundbreaking New York bill aims to redefine video game ownership. Instead of merely licensing digital games, consumers would gain actual ownership, akin to physical media. Publishers would also be obligated to ensure games remain playable even after official support ceases.

## ⚡ 5-Second Key Points
- **True Ownership**: Consumers would own games, not just license them.
- **Post-Support Playability**: Publishers must keep games functional after support ends.
- **Consumer Rights**: Aims to bolster digital consumer rights in the gaming sphere.

## 📈 Detailed Breakdown
**Shift from Licensing to Ownership**
The bill proposes a fundamental change in how digital games are acquired. Currently, players purchase licenses to access games, which can be revoked or altered by publishers. This legislation seeks to grant permanent ownership rights to consumers.

**Obligation for Continued Functionality**
A significant aspect is the requirement for publishers to maintain game functionality. This means games should remain playable, potentially through offline modes or server emulation, even after the publisher discontinues active support or servers.

> 💡 Insight: This could drastically reduce the risk of digital games becoming unplayable due to publisher decisions or server shutdowns.

## 🎯 Real-World Impact
- Increased consumer control and long-term access to purchased games.
- Potential for a more robust second-hand digital game market.
- Publishers may need to rethink game architecture and long-term support strategies.

## ✨ Conclusion
This New York bill represents a significant step towards empowering gamers with true ownership and ensuring the longevity of their digital libraries, potentially setting a new industry standard.
