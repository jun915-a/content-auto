## 🔑 The Core of This Topic
Sun Microsystems was a titan of innovation in the late 20th century, pioneering open-source software (Solaris, NetBeans), Java, and distributed computing. Yet, despite its groundbreaking contributions, the company collapsed in 2010 after being acquired by Oracle. The root cause wasn’t just market forces—it was **Sun’s inability to balance vision with pragmatism**, its **over-reliance on internal culture over external collaboration**, and its **failure to adapt to the shifting tech landscape**. This article dissects how Sun’s core flaws—**technical arrogance, fragmented leadership, and misplaced priorities**—doomed it, offering timeless lessons for modern tech firms.
