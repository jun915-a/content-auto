# The Doorman's Fallacy: Misunderstanding Probability

The Doorman's Fallacy is a cognitive bias that misinterprets random events as having a pattern. Learn how to spot it and avoid being misled.

## 🔑 The Core of This Topic
The Doorman's Fallacy is a cognitive bias that arises when people overestimate the probability of a random event happening again soon, thinking that the universe is more predictable than it really is.

## ⚡ 5-Second Key Points
- **Point 1**: This bias leads people to believe that a random event is more likely to happen again in the near future because it happened recently.
- **Point 2**: It's a common mistake that affects many areas of life, from finance to relationships.
- **Point 3**: By recognizing the Doorman's Fallacy, you can make more informed decisions and avoid unnecessary risks.

## 📈 Detailed Breakdown
**Random Events Are Just That**
Random events are unpredictable and don't follow a pattern, yet people often try to find connections between them.

**The Bias in Action**
Imagine you're a doorman and a person walks in every 10 minutes. You might think that 5 minutes later, another person will walk in, but this is just a coincidence.

> 💡 Insight: The Doorman's Fallacy is a result of our brain's tendency to seek patterns, even when there are none.

## 🎯 Real-World Impact
- **Financial Losses**: Investing in the stock market based on recent trends can lead to financial losses due to the Doorman's Fallacy.
- **Relationship Mistakes**: Misinterpreting random events in relationships can lead to unnecessary conflicts and misunderstandings.
- **Daily Decisions**: This bias can affect even everyday decisions, such as when to order food or what to wear.

## ✨ Conclusion
The Doorman's Fallacy is a common cognitive bias that can lead to misunderstandings and poor decision-making. By recognizing it, you can become more aware of your thought process and make more informed choices.
