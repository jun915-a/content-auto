# Facebook Pages: Why Link Limits Now Cost Money

Facebook’s new policy forces Pages to pay for posting more than two links per month. Discover the implications, workarounds, and why this change could reshape how developers and creators share content online.

## 🔑 The Core of This Topic
Facebook’s latest update to its **Page Posting Policy** now requires a paid subscription for Pages that exceed two link posts per month. This shift forces businesses, developers, and creators to either **pay for promotion** or **strictly limit their content distribution**, raising concerns about accessibility and organic reach.

## ⚡ 5-Second Key Points
- **Paid restrictions**: Pages must subscribe to Meta Business Suite to post more than two links monthly.
- **Developer impact**: Gamedev and tech creators face higher costs to share tutorials, updates, or resources.
- **Workarounds exist**: Alternative platforms and scheduling tools may mitigate the change.

## 📈 Detailed Breakdown
**Why the Change?**
Facebook’s move aligns with its broader push toward monetizing organic reach. By limiting free link posts, the platform incentivizes Pages to invest in ads, ensuring revenue from promotions rather than unpaid engagement. This affects **gamedev communities, indie devs, and tech-focused Pages** that rely on sharing links to demos, patches, or tutorials.

**How It Works**
- Pages with **free accounts** can post **up to two links per month** without restrictions.
- Exceeding this limit triggers a **paid subscription requirement** (~$5–$20/month via Meta Business Suite).
- **No exceptions**: Even educational or non-commercial Pages must comply.

> 💡 Insight: **This policy could accelerate migration** from Facebook to platforms like Mastodon, Discord, or Twitter, where link-sharing remains unrestricted.

**Alternatives for Creators**
- **Cross-posting**: Share updates on **Mastodon (e.g., @gamedev.place), Twitter, or Discord** to bypass limits.
- **Scheduling tools**: Use **Buffer or Hootsuite** to distribute links within the free limit.
- **Email newsletters**: Build direct audiences via **Mailchimp or Substack** for updates.

## 🎯 Real-World Impact
- **Higher costs for indie devs**: Smaller teams may struggle to afford promotions, reducing visibility.
- **Shift in content strategy**: Creators will prioritize **video or image posts** over link-heavy updates.
- **Trust erosion**: Users may perceive paid Pages as biased or less transparent.

## ✨ Conclusion
Facebook’s new policy isn’t just a technical tweak—it’s a **business decision** prioritizing revenue over organic reach. While workarounds exist, the shift underscores the platform’s evolving priorities. Creators must adapt by **diversifying their distribution channels** and considering whether Facebook remains the best home for their content.
