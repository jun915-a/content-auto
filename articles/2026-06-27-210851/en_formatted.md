# California Bans Loud Streaming Ads July 1

*Insert header image here*

Tired of jarringly loud ads interrupting your shows? California is making it illegal for streaming services to broadcast ads significantly louder than program content starting July 1.

## 🔑 The Core of This Topic
California is enacting a new law that prohibits streaming services from airing advertisements that are substantially louder than the program content. This aims to curb the common and annoying practice of sudden, loud ad bursts.

## ⚡ 5-Second Key Points
- **New Law**: California bans excessively loud streaming ads.
- **Effective Date**: The ban starts on July 1.
- **Consumer Relief**: Aims to stop jarring audio interruptions.

## 📈 Detailed Breakdown
**The Problem with Loud Ads**
For years, viewers have complained about ads that dramatically increase in volume compared to the shows they are watching, creating an unpleasant and disruptive experience that often startles audiences.

**The CALM Act Influence**
This California law is inspired by the federal CALM Act (Commercial Advertisement Loudness Mitigation Act) which regulates loudness in broadcast television, extending similar principles to the burgeoning streaming landscape.

> 💡 Insight: This move by California could set a precedent for other states and influence global standards for audio in digital media.

## 🎯 Real-World Impact
- Consumers will experience a more consistent and less jarring audio environment while streaming.
- Streaming platforms and advertisers will need to ensure ads comply with new loudness standards.
- Potential for improved viewer satisfaction and reduced ad avoidance.

## ✨ Conclusion
California's new law offers a much-needed reprieve from intrusive, loud advertising, promising a more enjoyable and less startling streaming experience for its residents.
