**Element 1**
The explosion was logged by the U.S. Geological Survey (USGS) as an **experimental detonation**, a classification used for human-made events rather than natural seismic occurrences. Such explosions are often tied to military testing, underwater demolition, or research activities. The shallow depth of the event (likely within a few kilometers of the surface) aligns with typical explosion profiles, which release energy more abruptly than slow tectonic shifts.

**Element 2**
Coastal communities in Florida, including Daytona Beach and Jacksonville, reported no immediate tremors or structural impacts. However, the event underscores the importance of monitoring offshore activities, especially given the region’s vulnerability to hurricanes and other natural disasters. The USGS has clarified that while the event was detected, it does not meet the criteria for a conventional earthquake, which relies on fault-based energy release.
