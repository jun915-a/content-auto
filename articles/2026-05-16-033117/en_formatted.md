## The Core of This Topic
The US Department of Justice (DOJ) has demanded that Apple and Google reveal the identities of over 100,000 users of a popular car-tinkering app, as part of a crackdown on vehicle emissions tampering.

## 5-Second Key Points
- **Emissions Crackdown**: The DOJ is targeting users who may be using the app to tamper with vehicle emissions.
- **User Data**: Apple and Google have been asked to hand over user data, including names and addresses.
- **Privacy Concerns**: The move has raised concerns about user privacy and data protection.

## Detailed Breakdown
**User Privacy**
The demand for user data has sparked concerns about the potential for mass surveillance and the erosion of user privacy.

**App Usage**
The car-tinkering app in question allows users to modify their vehicle's performance, which may be used to bypass emissions regulations.

> Insight: The DOJ's move highlights the tension between law enforcement's need for data and users' right to privacy.

## Real-World Impact
- Increased scrutiny of app usage and data collection
- Potential for widespread surveillance and privacy erosion
- Impact on the development of car-tinkering apps and related technologies

## Conclusion
The US DOJ's demand for user data has significant implications for user privacy and the tech industry as a whole, highlighting the need for a balanced approach to data protection and law enforcement.
