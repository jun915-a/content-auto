Despite being public since 2012, a staggering 68.4% of domains still don't enforce DMARC, leaving email services vulnerable to phishing and spam.

## 🔑 The Core of This Topic
DMARC, or Domain-based Message Authentication, Reporting, and Conformance, is a security protocol designed to prevent email spoofing and phishing attacks. It helps verify the authenticity of emails sent on behalf of a domain, reducing the risk of malicious emails being delivered to users' inboxes.

Despite its benefits, a significant portion of domains still don't enforce DMARC, leaving their email services vulnerable to attacks.
