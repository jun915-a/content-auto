White light has long fascinated humans, its composition and properties still a subject of scientific inquiry. But what exactly is white light, and how is it formed?

## 🔑 The Core of This Topic
White light is a spectral mixture of all visible colors, encompassing the entire visible spectrum from approximately 380 nanometers (violet) to 740 nanometers (red). This complex mixture is created when sunlight or any other light source is refracted through a prism or a similar medium, separating it into its constituent colors.

## ⚡ 5-Second Key Points
- **Point 1**: White light is composed of all visible colors.
- **Point 2**: It is created when sunlight is refracted through a prism.
- **Point 3**: The visible spectrum ranges from 380nm to 740nm.

## 📈 Detailed Breakdown
**Element 1**
The formation of white light is a result of the superposition of all the colors of the visible spectrum. When sunlight enters a prism, it is split into its individual colors due to the dispersion of light. This phenomenon is known as dispersion, and it occurs because each color of light has a unique wavelength and is refracted at a slightly different angle.

**Element 2**
The combination of these individual colors creates the sensation of white light. However, it's essential to note that true white light does not exist in nature. What we perceive as white light is actually a mixture of all the colors of the visible spectrum.
