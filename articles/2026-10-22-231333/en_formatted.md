## 📈 Detailed Breakdown
**Element 1**:
Researchers at **Frontiers in Artificial Intelligence** cultivated **3D brain organoids**—tiny clusters of neural cells grown from stem cells—that can **detect and classify Braille dots**. By embedding **electrodes** into the organoids, scientists trained them to **fire neural responses** in specific patterns when exposed to Braille stimuli. This mimics how **human sensory neurons** process tactile information, but in a controlled, lab-grown system.

**Element 2**:
The breakthrough hinges on **neural plasticity**—the organoids’ ability to **rewire themselves** based on input. Unlike rigid silicon chips, these living networks **adapt and improve** over time, potentially offering **self-learning capabilities**. The team also highlighted that this system could **reduce energy consumption** by orders of magnitude compared to traditional AI processors, as biological neurons operate far more efficiently.

> 💡 Insight: **This isn’t just computing—it’s a step toward 'living AI'**, where biological systems handle tasks like pattern recognition, memory, and even decision-making.
