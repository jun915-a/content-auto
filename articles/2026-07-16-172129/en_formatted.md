Sony has deleted more movies from user accounts, sparking concerns over digital ownership and consumer rights, affecting those who thought they 'bought' the films

## 🔑 The Core of This Topic
Sony's decision to remove movies from user accounts has raised questions about the nature of digital ownership, leaving many to wonder if they truly own the content they purchase.

## ⚡ 5-Second Key Points
- **Lack of Ownership**: Users do not actually own digital copies of movies
- **License Agreements**: Terms of service often include clauses allowing companies to remove content
- **Consumer Rights**: The incident highlights the need for clearer consumer protections

## 📈 Detailed Breakdown
**Digital Licenses**
The issue stems from the fact that when users 'buy' digital movies, they are actually purchasing a license to access the content, which can be revoked at any time.

**Company Policies**
Companies like Sony often have policies in place that allow them to remove content from user accounts, often due to licensing agreements or other issues.

> 💡 Insight: This incident serves as a reminder to carefully review the terms of service and understand what you are actually purchasing when you buy digital content.

## 🎯 Real-World Impact
- Users may lose access to movies they have purchased
- The incident undermines trust in digital marketplaces
- It highlights the need for greater transparency in licensing agreements
