# Photopea Dev Battles GitHub Over Stolen Code: A Fight for Digital Rights

When AI scrapers and malicious actors stole Photopea’s JavaScript code, the developer faced GitHub’s inaction—despite a DMCA takedown request. Here’s how one dev fought back and what it means for open-source integrity.

## 🔑 The Core of This Topic
A developer of **Photopea**, a popular browser-based photo editor, discovered their entire JavaScript codebase was being scraped, reverse-engineered, and redistributed as "cracked" versions on third-party platforms. After filing a **DMCA takedown request** with GitHub, the company ignored it for over a month, leaving the stolen code publicly accessible. This case highlights the **growing problem of automated theft** of open-source software and the **limited enforcement** by major platforms like GitHub.

## ⚡ 5-Second Key Points
- **AI scrapers** are systematically stealing JavaScript code from websites to create pirated tools.
- GitHub’s **DMCA takedown process is slow and unreliable**, even for blatant copyright violations.
- Developers like Photopea’s creator are **fighting back** with legal actions and community awareness.

## 📈 Detailed Breakdown
**Element 1**
Photopea, a **free, browser-based alternative to Adobe Photoshop**, relies entirely on open-source JavaScript. Its popularity—**millions of monthly users**—makes it a prime target for scrapers. AI tools like **GitHub Copilot** (and others) analyze public code to train models, but malicious actors repurpose it to **create cracked versions** of software, bypassing licensing protections. The developer noticed their code appearing in **pirated apps** on platforms like **APKMirror**, where users could download "free" versions of Photopea with ads or malware.

**Element 2**
When confronted, GitHub’s response was **slow and dismissive**. After submitting a **DMCA takedown request**, the stolen repositories remained **publicly accessible for over a month**, despite clear evidence of copyright infringement. This raises questions about **GitHub’s commitment to protecting developers**—especially when the theft involves **automated scraping** rather than direct piracy. The developer later resorted to **manual takedowns** and **legal threats** to force compliance, a process that should not be necessary for basic copyright enforcement.

> 💡 Insight: **GitHub’s takedown system fails when the infringement is systemic and automated**, leaving developers with few recourse options beyond legal action or public shaming.

## 🎯 Real-World Impact
- **Open-source developers lose revenue and credibility** when their work is repurposed without consent, often leading to **malware distribution** or **adware-laden forks**.
- **AI training on scraped code** raises ethical concerns—developers’ work is being used to **train models** without compensation or permission.
- **Platforms like GitHub must improve enforcement** or risk becoming **complicit in digital theft**, undermining trust in open-source ecosystems.

## ✨ Conclusion
This case is a **warning for all developers**: even if your code is open-source, **automated scraping and piracy are real threats**. GitHub’s inaction sends a dangerous signal—that **copyright protection is optional** when the infringement is large-scale. While legal recourse exists, the **burden should not fall entirely on developers** to police their own work. The solution? **Stronger platform policies**, **faster takedown responses**, and **community-driven accountability** to ensure that **open-source remains fair for everyone**.
