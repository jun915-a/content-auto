## 🔑 The Core of This Topic
Benford's Law is a mathematical concept that describes the distribution of digits in naturally occurring datasets. It states that the leading digit of numbers in these datasets follows a specific pattern, with 1 being the most common leading digit. This phenomenon has been observed in various fields, including finance, physics, and demographics.

## 📈 Detailed Breakdown
**Element 1**
The law was first discovered by Simon Newcomb in 1881 and later popularized by Frank Benford in 1938. Newcomb observed that the leading digit of numbers in logarithm tables follows a specific pattern, which Benford later verified in various datasets.

**Element 2**
The Benford's Law distribution is a result of the way numbers are generated and recorded in various fields. For example, in financial transactions, the leading digit of numbers is more likely to be 1 than 2, 3, or 4. This is because financial transactions often involve rounding numbers to the nearest dollar or cent.
