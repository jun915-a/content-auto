## 🔑 The Core of This Topic
Some Apollo missions were equipped with a device that resembled a grenade launcher, but it wasn't for combat. This tool was actually a **geological hammer** designed for collecting rock samples on the Moon. Its distinctive shape, similar to a launcher, was a functional design choice for its intended use.

## ⚡ 5-Second Key Points
- **Geological Tool**: It was a specialized hammer for sample collection.
- **Lunar Surface Use**: Designed for breaking and collecting moon rocks.
- **Misleading Appearance**: Its shape led to speculation about other uses.

## 📈 Detailed Breakdown
**The Lunar Sample Hammer**
This tool, officially called the Lunar Sample Hammer, was crucial for astronauts to gather valuable geological specimens. It allowed them to break off pieces of lunar rocks and place them into sample bags for return to Earth.

**Design and Functionality**
The hammer featured a long handle for reach and a weighted head. The 'launcher' like appearance likely stemmed from its robust construction and the need for leverage when striking harder lunar materials. It was built to withstand the harsh lunar environment.

> 💡 Insight: The primary function was scientific exploration, not defense or offense.
