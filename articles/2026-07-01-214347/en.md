# Temperature Tolls: Mortality from Non-Optimal Ambient Temperatures

A three-stage modelling study reveals alarming mortality rates linked to temperature extremes worldwide from 2000 to 2019.

## 🔑 The Core of This Topic
Global temperatures have been on the rise, with far-reaching implications for human health. Prolonged exposure to non-optimal ambient temperatures, both hot and cold, has been linked to increased mortality rates worldwide. This study aimed to quantify the global, regional, and national burden of mortality associated with non-optimal ambient temperatures from 2000 to 2019.

## ⚡ 5-Second Key Points
- **Point 1**: Exposure to temperature extremes contributes significantly to global mortality.
- **Point 2**: Hot temperatures are a major risk factor in low- and middle-income countries.
- **Point 3**: Climate change exacerbates temperature-related mortality.

## 📈 Detailed Breakdown
**Element 1**: The study employed a three-stage modelling approach to estimate mortality associated with non-optimal ambient temperatures. This involved estimating temperature-related mortality at the country level, followed by aggregating these estimates to the global and regional levels. The results showed that exposure to temperature extremes was a significant contributor to global mortality.

**Element 2**: The study found that hot temperatures were a major risk factor in low- and middle-income countries, while cold temperatures were more prevalent in high-income countries. Climate change was identified as a key driver of increasing temperature-related mortality.

> 💡 Insight: Temperature exposure is a critical health risk that warrants immediate attention, particularly in the context of climate change.

## 🎯 Real-World Impact
- Temperature-related mortality has significant economic and social implications, including strain on healthcare systems and loss of productivity.
- The findings of this study highlight the need for targeted interventions to mitigate the effects of temperature extremes, particularly in vulnerable populations.
- Climate change mitigation strategies, such as reducing greenhouse gas emissions, are essential for reducing temperature-related mortality.

## ✨ Conclusion
The results of this study underscore the critical need for global action to address the growing burden of temperature-related mortality. By understanding the magnitude and distribution of this risk factor, we can develop targeted interventions to protect vulnerable populations and reduce the toll of temperature extremes on human health.
