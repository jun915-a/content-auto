# Build a Pocket-Sized Mechanical TV That Fits in Your Bag

Imagine carrying a retro TV in your pocket—no batteries, no screens, just spinning mirrors and light. Here’s how to make a portable wide-screen mechanical TV at home.

  "## 📈 Detailed Breakdown\n**Element 1**\nA mechanical TV relies on the **Nipkow disk**, patented in 1884. This spinning disk has a spiral of holes that scan an image vertically as it rotates. For a portable version, use a lightweight acrylic disk with 30–50 holes drilled in a spiral pattern. The disk’s speed determines the frame rate—rotate it at 10–15 revolutions per second for smooth motion. Smaller disks (under 10 cm) fit in a pocket but require precise alignment to avoid distortion.\n\n**Element 2**\nTo create a **wide-screen effect**, mirrors are angled to stretch the image horizontally. Place a half-silvered mirror at 45 degrees in front of the Nipkow disk, reflecting light from a small LED or ambient source. Behind the disk, a second mirror redirects the scanned light onto a frosted glass or translucent screen. The angle between mirrors determines the aspect ratio—adjust it to mimic a 16:9 display. For portability, use lightweight aluminum-coated mylar mirrors or thin acrylic sheets with reflective coatings.\n\n> 💡 Insight: The key to a sharp image is **consistent disk speed** and **precise mirror alignment**. Even a slight wobble in rotation or misalignment will blur the picture.
