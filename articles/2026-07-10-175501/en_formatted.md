## 🔑 The Core of This Topic
The Terminator 2: Judgment Day's liquid metal T-1000 was a game-changer in visual effects, featuring a unique blend of practical and computer-generated imagery. This article delves into the technology behind this iconic character and the impact it had on the industry.

## ⚡ 5-Second Key Points
* **Point 1**: The film's visual effects were a result of a collaboration between ILM, Stan Winston's Studio, and the in-house effects team at 20th Century Fox.
* **Point 2**: The T-1000's liquid metal appearance was achieved through a combination of practical and digital effects.
* **Point 3**: The film's use of 3D computer-generated imagery was a significant departure from traditional techniques.

## 📈 Detailed Breakdown
**Practical Effects**
The T-1000's liquid metal appearance was achieved through a combination of silicone and liquid latex. Stan Winston's Studio created the practical effects, including the T-1000's endoskeleton.

**Digital Effects**
The digital effects were handled by ILM, who used a combination of 2D and 3D computer-generated imagery to create the T-1000's liquid metal appearance. The 3D computer-generated imagery was a significant departure from traditional techniques and paved the way for future advancements in visual effects.

> 💡 Insight: The T-1000's liquid metal appearance has become an iconic symbol of the Terminator franchise and a benchmark for visual effects in film.
