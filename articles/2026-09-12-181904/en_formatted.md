## 🔑 The Core of This Topic
Great Lakes sturgeon, some of the oldest living fish on Earth, may reach **400 years of age**—yet they face extinction. A groundbreaking study reveals their astonishing lifespan, raising urgent questions about how humans can protect them before it’s too late.

## ⚡ 5-Second Key Points
- **Point 1**: Sturgeon in Lake Ontario live **400+ years**, far longer than previously estimated (150 years).
- **Point 2**: Overfishing, habitat destruction, and pollution have devastated their populations by **90%+** in the past century.
- **Point 3**: Scientists now advocate for **radical conservation shifts**, including stricter fishing bans and river restoration.

## 📈 Detailed Breakdown
**Element 1**
Researchers at the **University of Toronto** used **age estimation techniques** (like vertebrae analysis) to confirm sturgeon can live **four centuries**—longer than any other known fish. A 400-year-old sturgeon would have swum alongside **King Louis XIV of France** and witnessed the **American Revolution**. Yet, despite their longevity, these prehistoric fish are **critically endangered** in the Great Lakes.

**Element 2**
The decline is **human-driven**: industrial fishing in the 1800s nearly wiped them out, and modern threats like **dams, invasive species, and water pollution** (e.g., PFAS chemicals) cripple their survival. A 2023 study found **only 1% of historic sturgeon populations remain** in Lake Ontario, where they once thrived.

> 💡 Insight: **Longevity is their curse**—slow reproduction (they mature at **15+ years**) means recovery takes centuries, but humans alter ecosystems in decades.

## 🎯 Real-World Impact
- **Fisheries collapse**: Sturgeon are a **keystone species**; their decline disrupts food webs, affecting trout, salmon, and even birds.
- **Cultural loss**: Indigenous communities like the **Anishinaabe** relied on sturgeon for **food, medicine, and ceremonies**—their disappearance erases centuries of tradition.
- **Climate warning**: Sturgeon are **biological indicators** of ecosystem health; their extinction signals deeper environmental crises.

## ✨ Conclusion
The Great Lakes sturgeon’s story is a **tragedy of time and human shortsightedness**. While science now confirms their **unprecedented lifespan**, conservationists must act faster than the species can reproduce. The choice is clear: **protect them now**, or lose a living relic of Earth’s ancient past forever.
