## 🔑 The Core of This Topic
The question of how many elementary particles exist is a cornerstone of modern physics, with far-reaching implications for our understanding of the universe. The Standard Model of particle physics, which describes the behavior of subatomic particles, identifies a handful of elementary particles. However, new discoveries and theoretical frameworks have raised questions about the nature of these particles and the possibility of new, undiscovered particles.

## 📈 Detailed Breakdown
**Protons**
Protons are among the most well-known elementary particles, making up the nucleus of an atom along with neutrons. However, their status as elementary particles has been questioned due to the discovery of quarks, which are the fundamental constituents of protons.

**Quarks**
Quarks are the building blocks of protons and neutrons, and they come in six 'flavors': up, down, charm, strange, top, and bottom. However, the existence of quarks has raised questions about the nature of elementary particles, as they can combine to form composite particles.

> 💡 Insight: The line between elementary and composite particles is not always clear-cut, and new discoveries may challenge our understanding of the fundamental building blocks of matter.
