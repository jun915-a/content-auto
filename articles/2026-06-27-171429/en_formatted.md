## The Core of This Topic
Kinetic energy increases quadratically with speed due to the work done by a force over a distance, which depends on the object's mass and the distance it travels.

## 5-Second Key Points
- **Point 1**: Kinetic energy depends on mass and velocity
- **Point 2**: Quadratic relationship with speed
- **Point 3**: Derived from the work-energy principle

## Detailed Breakdown
**Element 1**
The work-energy principle states that the net work done on an object is equal to its change in kinetic energy. As an object moves, the force applied to it does work, increasing its kinetic energy.

**Element 2**
The distance an object travels is directly related to its velocity. As velocity increases, the distance traveled in a given time also increases, resulting in more work done and a greater increase in kinetic energy.

> Insight: The quadratic relationship between kinetic energy and speed is a fundamental consequence of the work-energy principle.
