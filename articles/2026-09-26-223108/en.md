# Conversations App Goes Free: Goodbye Google Play Fees!

Discover why the popular open-source messaging app, Conversations, is ditching Google Play. Learn about their new model and what it means for users and developers.

## 🔑 The Core of This Topic
Conversations, a leading open-source XMPP client, has moved away from the Google Play Store for its primary distribution. This shift is to avoid the 15% or 30% "tax" Google imposes on in-app purchases, allowing the developers to offer the app for free to all users.

## ⚡ 5-Second Key Points
- **Free App**: Conversations is now completely free for everyone.
- **Play Store Exit**: Developers left Google Play to avoid fees.
- **Direct Downloads**: Users can get the app directly from the developer's site.

## 📈 Detailed Breakdown
**No More Google Play Fees**
The primary driver for this decision is the financial burden of Google Play's commission fees. By distributing elsewhere, Conversations can retain all revenue and offer the app without the Play Store's cut.

**Alternative Distribution Channels**
The app is now available directly from the developer's website, F-Droid, and other platforms that do not charge distribution fees. This empowers developers and offers users more choices.

> 💡 Insight: Avoiding platform fees allows open-source projects to be more sustainable and accessible.

**Focus on User Choice**
This move emphasizes user freedom and developer autonomy, moving away from a centralized, fee-based ecosystem towards a more open and direct relationship with the community.

## 🎯 Real-World Impact
- Users can now download Conversations for free without any in-app purchase requirement.
- Developers can better sustain the project without paying significant fees to app stores.
- The move encourages alternative app distribution models, promoting competition and innovation.

## ✨ Conclusion
Conversations' departure from Google Play marks a significant step for open-source apps seeking financial independence and direct user engagement. It's a win for users and developers alike, fostering a more open app ecosystem.
