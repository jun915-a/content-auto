# Still Running Businesses on DOS? HN Discusses Legacy Systems

Hacker News thread reveals businesses still reliant on DOS machines and old RAD environments like dBase and Clipper for critical operations. Discover why.

## 🔑 The Core of This Topic
Many businesses, particularly smaller or niche ones, continue to operate using critical software developed on DOS and maintained on period hardware. This reliance stems from custom-built applications that are too expensive or risky to migrate.

## ⚡ 5-Second Key Points
- **Legacy Dependence**: Businesses stick with DOS due to critical, custom-built software.
- **High Migration Costs**: Replacing old systems is prohibitively expensive and risky.
- **Niche Applications**: Some specialized software is simply unavailable on modern platforms.

## 📈 Detailed Breakdown
**DOS-Based Business Software**
Many companies developed proprietary software in environments like dBase, Clipper, or Clarion on DOS. These applications, often years or decades old, are still the backbone of their operations, from inventory to accounting.

**Hardware Obsolescence**
Maintaining the original period hardware is a significant challenge. Finding replacement parts or compatible components for these aging machines requires specialized knowledge and often scavenging.

> 💡 Insight: The cost and risk of migrating deeply embedded, custom DOS software often outweigh the perceived benefits of modernization for many small businesses.

## 🎯 Real-World Impact
- Businesses face challenges in finding skilled personnel to maintain DOS systems.
- Security vulnerabilities are a constant concern with outdated operating systems.
- Integration with modern systems is often complex or impossible.

## ✨ Conclusion
The thread highlights the surprising resilience of old technology in business, driven by cost, necessity, and the inertia of deeply integrated legacy systems.
