## 📈 Detailed Breakdown
**Element 1**
Axon’s **ALPR (Automatic License Plate Reader) cameras** are deployed in public spaces like intersections, parking lots, and highways. Unlike traditional speed cameras, these devices **scan every vehicle** passing by, storing data in cloud databases. The technology is often **bundled with police body cams**, raising concerns about **mass surveillance** and **privacy erosion**. Cities may claim these tools are for **traffic enforcement**, but critics argue they enable **profiling and tracking** without clear legal safeguards.

**Element 2**
The **lack of transparency** is alarming. Many jurisdictions **don’t disclose** their use of Axon cameras, leaving residents unaware of how their movements are being recorded. Worse, some departments **share data with third parties**, including private companies or federal agencies, without public consent. This **blurs the line between security and surveillance**, turning everyday driving into a **digital footprint** that could be exploited.

> 💡 Insight: **If your city isn’t openly discussing ALPR use, it’s likely hiding something.**
