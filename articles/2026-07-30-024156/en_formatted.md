## 🔑 The Core of This Topic
Recursive filters are a type of signal processing technique used to smooth out noise and extract valuable insights from data. They work by recursively applying a mathematical formula to each data point, resulting in a smoothed output.

## ⚡ 5-Second Key Points
- **Point 1**: Simple Moving Average (SMA) is a basic recursive filter that calculates the mean of a fixed number of previous values.
- **Point 2**: Exponential Moving Average (EMA) is a weighted average of previous values, giving more importance to recent data.
- **Point 3**: Low-Pass filters remove high-frequency noise by emphasizing lower frequencies.
- **Point 4**: The Tiny Kalman filter is a simplified version of the Kalman filter, suitable for small datasets or real-time applications.

## 📈 Detailed Breakdown
**SMA**
The Simple Moving Average (SMA) is a basic recursive filter that calculates the mean of a fixed number of previous values. It's useful for removing random noise and highlighting trends in data.

**EMA**
The Exponential Moving Average (EMA) is a weighted average of previous values, giving more importance to recent data. This makes it more responsive to changes in the data.

> 💡 Insight: Recursive filters can be used to smooth out noise and extract valuable insights from data, but choosing the right filter depends on the specific application and data characteristics.

## 🎯 Real-World Impact
- Recursive filters are widely used in finance to smooth out stock prices and extract trends.
- They're also used in engineering to remove noise from sensor data and improve the accuracy of measurements.
- In data science, recursive filters are used to preprocess data before applying machine learning algorithms.

## ✨ Conclusion
Recursive filters are a powerful tool for signal processing, allowing us to smooth out noise and extract valuable insights from data. By understanding the different types of recursive filters and their applications, we can make more informed decisions and improve the accuracy of our models.
