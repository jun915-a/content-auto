## 🔑 The Core of This Topic
Microsoft’s new Outlook for Windows has introduced frustrating delays, with some users experiencing up to **10-second lags** for actions that Outlook Classic completes **instantly**. This performance regression has sparked widespread frustration among professionals relying on the email client for daily productivity.

## ⚡ 5-Second Key Points
- **Instant vs. Instantly**: Outlook Classic executes tasks like switching folders or opening emails **in a fraction of a second**, while the new version often stalls for **10+ seconds**. 
- **Performance Regression**: Users upgrading from Outlook Classic to the new Outlook report **consistent slowdowns**, particularly on Windows systems.
- **Microsoft’s Stance**: Despite complaints, Microsoft has not addressed the issue publicly, leaving users in limbo.
- **Workaround Frustration**: Some users have reverted to Outlook Classic due to the **unusable delays**, defeating the purpose of the "modern" upgrade.
- **Industry Impact**: The sluggish performance undermines trust in Microsoft’s email solutions, especially for enterprise users.

## 📈 Detailed Breakdown
**Element 1**
The new Outlook’s architecture, built on **Electron and web technologies**, introduces significant overhead compared to the decades-old **native C++ code** of Outlook Classic. While Microsoft touts a unified experience, the trade-off appears to be **crippling latency** for basic operations. Users migrating from Outlook Classic—especially those on **older hardware**—are the hardest hit, as their systems struggle with the new app’s resource demands.

**Element 2**
Reports indicate that even **simple actions** like opening an email or navigating between folders now require **multiple seconds of waiting**, disrupting workflows in critical scenarios. The delays seem to worsen with **larger mailbox sizes**, suggesting inefficiencies in how the new Outlook handles data caching and rendering. Microsoft’s silence on the matter has only fueled speculation that the issue stems from **rushed development** or **prioritizing aesthetics over performance**.

> 💡 Insight: The new Outlook’s performance issues highlight a **fundamental flaw in Microsoft’s modern app strategy**—sacrificing user experience for cross-platform compatibility. This could erode confidence in Microsoft’s ability to maintain core productivity tools.
