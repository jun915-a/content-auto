# France to Block PQC-Free Products from Certification in 2027

## 🔑 The Core of This Topic
France's Agence Nationale de la Sécurité des Systèmes d'Information (ANSSI) will block products without post-quantum cryptography (PQC) from certification starting 2027. This move aims to secure French digital infrastructure against potential quantum computer threats.

## ⚡ 5-Second Key Points
- **Point 1**: ANSSI requires PQC in certified products to counter quantum threats.
- **Point 2**: Only products with PQC will be eligible for certification starting 2027.
- **Point 3**: The move is intended to protect French digital infrastructure.

## 📈 Detailed Breakdown
**Post-Quantum Cryptography (PQC) Requirements**: 
ANSSI's decision is driven by the growing threat of quantum computers, which could potentially break current encryption algorithms. To address this risk, ANSSI will only certify products that incorporate PQC solutions. This ensures that French digital infrastructure remains secure against potential quantum attacks.

**Impact on Industry**: 
The introduction of PQC requirements will have significant implications for industries that rely on secure digital infrastructure, including finance, healthcare, and government agencies. Companies must now ensure that their products and services are PQC-enabled to remain eligible for certification.

> 💡 Insight: 
By mandating PQC in certified products, ANSSI is taking proactive steps to safeguard French digital infrastructure against the looming threat of quantum computers.

## 🎯 Real-World Impact
- **Impact 1**: Increased adoption of PQC solutions across industries.
- **Impact 2**: Improved digital security for French citizens and organizations.
- **Impact 3**: Potential job creation in the PQC development and implementation sector.

## ✨ Conclusion
The ANSSI's decision to require PQC in certified products starting 2027 marks a significant step towards securing French digital infrastructure against potential quantum threats. As the threat landscape evolves, this move serves as a model for other countries to follow suit, safeguarding global digital security.
