## 📈 Detailed Breakdown
**Element 1**
The reviewer’s initial excitement for Framework’s modular design quickly turned to frustration. While the idea of swapping components like a LEGO set is appealing, the execution is far from seamless. The reviewer highlights how even minor repairs, like fixing a cracked screen, become a **multi-hour ordeal** with questionable results. The modularity, once a selling point, now feels like a **gimmick** that adds unnecessary complexity without real benefit.

**Element 2**
Customer support emerges as a **major failing point**. The reviewer recounts multiple instances where Framework’s support team was **unresponsive or dismissive**, leaving them to fend for themselves when hardware failed. Delays in repairs—sometimes stretching **weeks or months**—are particularly infuriating, especially when the laptop is under warranty. The reviewer also notes that **replacement parts are scarce**, forcing them to rely on third-party vendors, which often leads to **incompatible or low-quality replacements**. This lack of reliability undermines the entire modular philosophy, turning what should be a **consumer-friendly** product into a **frustrating headache**.

> 💡 Insight: **Modularity isn’t just about swapping parts—it’s about ensuring those parts are accessible, reliable, and supported.** Framework’s failure here isn’t just a product issue; it’s a **trust issue** with the brand itself.

> 💡 Insight: **Tech brands must balance innovation with usability.** Modularity is a fantastic concept, but it’s only valuable if it’s **practical, reliable, and supported**—not just a marketing ploy.
