## 🔑 The Core of This Topic
Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelensky has accused Russia of expanding its military strikes to include deliberate attacks on Ukraine’s data centres, a critical escalation in the war’s digital battlefield. These facilities house vital infrastructure for government operations, military coordination, and civilian services—making them prime targets in modern warfare. The attacks raise urgent concerns about Ukraine’s ability to maintain communication networks, financial systems, and emergency response capabilities amid ongoing hostilities.

## ⚡ 5-Second Key Points

## 📈 Detailed Breakdown
**Element 1**
The escalation comes as Ukraine faces relentless Russian strikes on energy grids, hospitals, and transportation hubs. By now targeting data centres—often overlooked in traditional warfare—Russia aims to **paralyze Ukraine’s digital backbone**. These facilities store and process data for everything from banking transactions to military logistics. A successful assault could lead to prolonged outages, leaving civilians and authorities vulnerable. Zelensky’s remarks highlight how modern conflicts increasingly rely on **disrupting technology** to weaken an enemy’s operational capacity.

**Element 2**
Experts warn that data centre attacks could have **domino effects**, cascading into broader disruptions. For instance, a compromised facility might expose sensitive government data or hinder Ukraine’s ability to coordinate defense efforts. Meanwhile, civilian access to essential services—like healthcare records or financial systems—could be severely limited. Ukraine’s reliance on Western support for cybersecurity measures becomes even more critical as Russia refines its **dual-pronged strategy** of physical destruction and digital sabotage.
