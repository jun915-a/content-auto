# Windows 11 Media Player Ram Hog & Codec Fees Spark Outrage

Windows 11's new Media Player is reportedly consuming 3.5x more RAM than its predecessor. Worse, Microsoft is rumored to be charging for popular video codecs, a move that has angered users.

## 🔑 The Core of This Topic
Microsoft's latest Windows 11 Media Player has come under fire for its significantly increased RAM usage, reportedly 3.5 times higher than the legacy Windows Media Player. Additionally, concerns are mounting over alleged charges for popular video codecs previously available for free.

## ⚡ 5-Second Key Points
- **Increased RAM Usage**: New player consumes significantly more memory.
- **Codec Costs**: Microsoft may charge for essential video formats.
- **User Backlash**: Community expresses frustration over these changes.

## 📈 Detailed Breakdown
**RAM Consumption Spike**
Early reports and user tests indicate that the new Media Player in Windows 11 demands a substantially larger memory footprint. This increase, cited as up to 3.5 times more RAM, could impact system performance, especially on lower-spec machines.

**Premium Codec Strategy**
Rumors suggest Microsoft plans to monetize common video codecs, such as HEVC, which were previously accessible. This shift could lead to extra costs for users wanting to play certain video files, a departure from established norms.

> 💡 Insight: This dual issue of resource drain and potential cost increases for basic functionality is a major concern for Windows users.

## 🎯 Real-World Impact
- **Performance Degradation**: Users with limited RAM may experience slower system performance.
- **Increased Software Costs**: Consumers might need to pay extra for video playback capabilities.
- **Community Dissatisfaction**: This move could damage user trust and lead to a search for alternative media players.

## ✨ Conclusion
Microsoft's new Media Player strategy appears to be a step backward in terms of efficiency and user-friendliness, potentially alienating its user base with increased resource demands and unexpected costs.
