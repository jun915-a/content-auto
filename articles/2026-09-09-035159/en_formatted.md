## 🔑 The Core of This Topic

This GitHub project, **`i-have-adhd`**, introduces a clever technique to **force AI agents to deliver answers concisely**—especially when they default to verbose or convoluted responses. By embedding a **simple prompt constraint** (e.g., `Answer in 3 bullet points or fewer`), users with ADHD (or anyone tired of rambling AI) can **cut through noise** and get to the heart of the matter faster. The trick? **Leveraging the agent’s own structure** to align with human attention spans.

## ⚡ 5-Second Key Points
- **Point 1**: Use **strict output formats** (e.g., bullet lists, numbered steps) to **force brevity** from AI agents.
- **Point 2**: The project demonstrates how **ADHD-friendly prompts** can **reduce cognitive overload** by limiting response length.
- **Point 3**: Works for **any AI tool**—from coding assistants to chatbots—by **imposing artificial constraints**.

## 📈 Detailed Breakdown

**Element 1**

The core idea is **reverse-engineering AI behavior**. Most AI agents, when left unchecked, **expand on every idea**—a trait that can feel like **digital ADHD overload** for users who struggle with focus. By **explicitly limiting output size** (e.g., `Answer in 3 sentences max`), you **train the agent to prioritize clarity over verbosity**. This isn’t about restricting creativity; it’s about **matching the user’s cognitive bandwidth**. For ADHD brains, where **hyperfocus often clashes with task-switching fatigue**, this method ensures answers are **digestible at a glance**.

**Element 2**

The project’s simplicity is its strength. No complex workflows—just **a single, reusable prompt template** that can be dropped into any AI interaction. For example:
- Instead of asking, *“How do I debug this error?”* try: *“Explain the bug in 2 bullet points and suggest 1 quick fix.”*

> 💡 Insight: **The constraint doesn’t limit depth—it forces precision**. If an AI tries to over-explain, the user can **gently redirect** with, *“Can you summarize that in 1 line?”* This turns **passive frustration** into an **active conversation** about clarity.

- **Empowerment**: Users regain control over **how information is delivered**, reducing the feeling of being “lost in the weeds.”
