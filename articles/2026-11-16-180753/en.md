# Amazon's Black Friday 'Deals' Misled Shoppers, Report Claims

Groundwork Collaborative investigation reveals a shocking truth: nearly one-third of Amazon's Black Friday and Cyber Monday 'deals' offered no actual savings, with some even increasing in price.

## 🔑 The Core of This Topic
A recent investigation by Groundwork Collaborative alleges that a significant portion of Amazon's advertised Black Friday and Cyber Monday deals provided no genuine savings to consumers. This practice, dubbed 'decoy deals,' misleads shoppers into believing they are getting discounts when prices may have been inflated beforehand.

## ⚡ 5-Second Key Points
- **No Real Savings**: Almost 1 in 3 "deals" offered no discount.
- **Price Hikes**: Nearly 1 in 10 deals actually cost more.
- **Deceptive Marketing**: Amazon accused of misleading shoppers.

## 📈 Detailed Breakdown
**Inflated Base Prices**
Groundwork's analysis suggests that Amazon may have artificially inflated the original prices of items shortly before the sales events. This makes the subsequent 'discounted' price appear more significant than it actually is, creating a false sense of savings for consumers.

**Hidden Price Increases**
Beyond simply offering no savings, the investigation found that some advertised deals were priced higher than they were just days before the sale. This represents a direct deception, as consumers were led to believe they were purchasing an item at a reduced price when it was, in fact, more expensive.

> 💡 Insight: This tactic exploits consumer trust and the urgency of holiday shopping to drive sales through deception rather than genuine value.

## 🎯 Real-World Impact
- Consumers were tricked into thinking they were saving money, potentially overspending.
- Erosion of trust in online retailers and advertised discounts.
- Potential legal and regulatory scrutiny for deceptive advertising practices.

## ✨ Conclusion
This report raises serious questions about the integrity of Amazon's promotional pricing strategies, urging consumers to remain vigilant and critically evaluate advertised deals during major sales events.
