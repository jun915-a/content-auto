## 🔑 The Core of This Topic
Wombats possess a highly elastic digestive tract with varying stiffness in its walls. This unique anatomy allows their feces to be shaped into cubes during the final stages of digestion, a process influenced by the material's dehydration and passage.

## ⚡ 5-Second Key Points
- **Unique Intestines**: Wombats have specialized intestines that shape their poop.
- **Varying Elasticity**: Different sections of the intestine have different stiffnesses.
- **Dehydration Process**: Feces dry out and take shape as they move through the gut.

## 📈 Detailed Breakdown
**Intestinal Walls**
The key lies in the wombat's intestines, which have sections with varying degrees of elasticity. The front part is more elastic, while the back section is stiffer, allowing for the formation of flat sides and corners.

**Dehydration and Compression**
As the wombat's food moves through its digestive system, it becomes increasingly dehydrated. The varying pressure and stiffness of the intestinal walls during this final drying phase sculpt the feces into distinct cubes.

> 💡 Insight: The shape isn't just for show; it prevents the droppings from rolling away, aiding in territorial marking.
