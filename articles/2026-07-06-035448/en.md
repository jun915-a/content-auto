# NES Wobble: The Mystery of Composite Video Explained

Ever wonder why your NES's picture wobbles? It's all about how the console generates composite video signals and how TVs interpret them. Dive into the technical details!

## 🔑 The Core of This Topic
The NES's composite video signal is inherently unstable due to how it synchronizes video lines. This causes a slight, constant "wobble" as the TV struggles to perfectly align each scanline.

## ⚡ 5-Second Key Points
- **Signal Timing**: NES video timing isn't perfectly stable, causing scanline jitter.
- **TV Interpretation**: TVs have tolerance, but some are more sensitive to these imperfections.
- **Composite Artifacts**: The nature of composite video itself can amplify these timing issues.

## 📈 Detailed Breakdown
**NES Video Generation**
The NES generates its video signal by drawing each line sequentially. However, the timing between these lines isn't perfectly consistent, leading to minor variations in their vertical position.

**TV Scanline Synchronization**
Televisions synchronize their electron beam to draw scanlines based on timing signals from the video source. Imperfect NES timing makes perfect synchronization challenging, resulting in the visible wobble.

> 💡 Insight: The "wobble" is a side effect of the NES's unique, less precise video output method.

## 🎯 Real-World Impact
- Visual artifacts like screen wobble on older consoles.
- Understanding limitations of early video encoding techniques.
- Appreciation for how modern video signals achieve greater stability.

## ✨ Conclusion
The NES's characteristic wobble is a fascinating glimpse into the analog video era, showcasing the delicate balance between console output and TV reception.
