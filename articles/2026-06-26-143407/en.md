# 2,000 Attempts to Hack My AI Assistant: What Happened

Fernando I's AI assistant, Claw, was put to the test with 2,000 hacking attempts, revealing surprising insights and vulnerabilities.

## 🔑 The Core of This Topic

A security experiment like no other was conducted on my AI assistant, Claw, when 2,000 individuals attempted to hack it. The goal was to identify vulnerabilities and strengthen security measures.

## ⚡ 5-Second Key Points
- **Point 1**: Overwhelming number of attacks revealed a mix of successful and failed attempts.
- **Point 2**: Analysis showed most attacks were automated scripts.
- **Point 3**: Unexpectedly, human attackers were able to breach security.

## 📈 Detailed Breakdown
**Element 1**
The sheer volume of attempts, 2,000, put a significant strain on Claw's systems. This led to a mix of successful and failed attacks, resulting in a compromised system.

**Element 2**
A closer inspection revealed the majority of attacks were automated scripts designed to exploit common vulnerabilities. However, human attackers proved to be a more formidable foe.

> 💡 Insight: Human attackers are often more unpredictable and can find creative ways to breach systems.

## 🎯 Real-World Impact
- AI assistants are not invincible and can be vulnerable to attacks.
- Human attackers can be more effective than automated scripts.
- Strengthening security measures is crucial for protecting AI systems.

## ✨ Conclusion
The experiment provided valuable insights into the security of AI assistants like Claw. By understanding the types of attacks and vulnerabilities, we can work towards creating more secure systems that protect users' data and prevent potential breaches.
