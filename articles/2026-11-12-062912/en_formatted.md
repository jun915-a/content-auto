**📈 Detailed Breakdown**

**WebGL2’s Role in Realism**
WebGL2’s compute shaders and advanced rendering techniques allow for precise simulation of sand’s granular physics. Unlike traditional 2D sandboxes, this system models particles as 3D volumes, capturing granular cohesion, erosion, and fluid-like flow. The result? A digital sand that responds to gravity, collisions, and user input with near-physical accuracy. This leap forward enables artists and developers to create environments where sand behaves organically—like real-world terrain.

**Particle-Based Rendering**
The simulation relies on a **massive particle system**, where each grain of sand is individually tracked. WebGL2’s **vertex buffer objects (VBOs)** and **instanced rendering** optimize performance, ensuring smooth interactions even with dense sand piles. Techniques like **coherent early depth testing** and **dynamic lighting** further enhance realism, making shadows and reflections adapt dynamically to user actions. This approach eliminates the artificiality of pixelated or blocky sand, delivering a seamless, fluid experience.

> 💡 **Insight**: The fusion of **compute shaders** (for physics) and **fragment shaders** (for rendering) creates a feedback loop—physics updates influence visuals in real time, and lighting adjustments subtly alter particle behavior, deepening immersion.
