# Molly (Signal Fork) Vulnerable: Urgent Security Update Needed

Molly, a Signal-based fork, has been stagnant for three months, leaving users exposed to unpatched vulnerabilities. Discover the risks, why this matters, and what you can do.

## 🔑 The Core of This Topic
Molly, a decentralized messaging fork of Signal, has fallen into a critical security limbo. With no updates for **three months**, its user base remains vulnerable to exploits, patch gaps, and potential data breaches—all while competitors like Session or Briar continue evolving. This isn’t just a technical oversight; it’s a **security risk** with real-world consequences for privacy-conscious users.


## ⚡ 5-Second Key Points
- **No updates for 3 months**: Molly’s last release predates critical Signal patches.
- **Unpatched vulnerabilities**: Exploitable flaws in encryption or protocol could expose messages.
- **User distrust**: Stagnation erodes confidence in privacy-focused apps.


## 📈 Detailed Breakdown
**Element 1**
Molly’s last commit dates back to **October 2023**, while Signal itself has rolled out **multiple security updates** since then—including fixes for **zero-day exploits** and protocol refinements. Without active development, Molly’s codebase risks falling behind, leaving users dependent on outdated defenses. Worse, **third-party forks** (like Signal’s own) may introduce their own vulnerabilities if not rigorously audited.

**Element 2**
The lack of updates isn’t just about missing features—it’s about **security hygiene**. Modern messaging apps face **quantum-resistant encryption threats**, **SIM-swapping attacks**, and **backdoor risks**. Molly’s inactivity means users are **effectively relying on a static firewall** in a constantly shifting threat landscape. Even if the app’s core is secure, **dependency updates** (e.g., OpenSSL, libsignal) could introduce hidden risks.

> 💡 Insight: **Privacy isn’t static.** Apps that stop evolving become **liabilities**, not tools. Molly’s users must weigh the trade-off between decentralization and security.


## 🎯 Real-World Impact
- **Exposed communications**: Unpatched flaws could allow **message interception** or **metadata leaks**, undermining Signal’s core promise.
- **Trust erosion**: Users may abandon Molly for alternatives like **Session (E2E-encrypted, actively maintained)** or **Briar (offline-first, decentralized).**
- **Legal/regulatory risks**: Stagnant apps may fail compliance audits, leaving users vulnerable to **government subpoenas** or **data retention laws**.


## ✨ Conclusion
Molly’s stagnation is a **warning shot** for the decentralized messaging ecosystem. While forks like Molly offer **theoretical freedom**, they demand **active maintenance** to stay secure. Users should:
- **Audit alternatives** (Session, Briar) before relying on unpatched apps.
- **Demand transparency** from developers—security isn’t optional.
- **Prepare for migration** if Molly remains inactive beyond **Q1 2024**.

Privacy isn’t free—it requires **constant vigilance**. Molly’s case proves that **decentralization without updates is just another lock with a rusted key.**
