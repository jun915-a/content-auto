In the mid-1990s, James Cameron's Terminator 2: Judgment Day pushed the boundaries of visual effects and technological innovations, setting a new standard for the sci-fi genre.

## ⚡ 5-Second Key Points
- **Point 1**: The film employed advanced computer-generated imagery (CGI) and liquid-metal effects to bring the T-1000 to life.
- **Point 2**: The Terminator's endoskeleton was designed using advanced 3D modeling and computer animation techniques.
- **Point 3**: The film's success spawned a new era of practical and visual effects in the film industry.

## 📈 Detailed Breakdown
**CGI and the T-1000**
The T-1000's liquid-metal appearance was achieved through a combination of CGI and practical effects. The team developed a unique process to create the T-1000's surface, which involved pouring a liquid metal alloy into a mold and then manipulating it using a combination of heat and vacuum pressure.

**3D Modeling and Animation**
The Terminator's endoskeleton was designed using advanced 3D modeling and computer animation techniques. The team created a detailed model of the skeleton, which was then animated using a combination of keyframe animation and motion capture.

> 💡 Insight: The success of Terminator 2's visual effects paved the way for the widespread use of CGI in the film industry, transforming the way movies are made and changing the face of the industry forever.

- The film's success led to the development of new technologies and techniques that are still used in the film industry today.
