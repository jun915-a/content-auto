## 📈 Detailed Breakdown
**Coordinated Universal Time (UTC)**
The UTC time standard is a modern continuation of Greenwich Mean Time (GMT), which was established in 1675. The IERS is responsible for maintaining the stability of the UTC time standard by introducing leap seconds as necessary.

**Leap Seconds and Their Impact**
Leap seconds are introduced to account for the Earth's slightly irregular rotation period. On average, the Earth takes 24 hours to rotate once, but due to its slightly ellipsoidal shape and the way it wobbles on its axis, the rotation period can vary. Leap seconds are added to the UTC time standard to keep it in sync with the Earth's rotation.
