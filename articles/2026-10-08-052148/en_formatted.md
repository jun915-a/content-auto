## 📈 Detailed Breakdown
**The WebAssembly Revolution**
WebAssembly (WASM) compiles low-level languages like C/C++ into a near-native binary format, enabling **blazing-fast execution** in browsers. By recompiling the PSP version of *God of War*—originally written for PlayStation Portable’s hardware—snuri00 leveraged WASM’s ability to **mimic x86-like instructions**, making it run as if it were a modern web app. This avoids the overhead of emulators while preserving the game’s original codebase.

**Why This Matters for Gamers**
Most retro games require emulators, which often introduce lag or graphical inconsistencies. WASM eliminates this by **directly translating the game’s binary** into a format browsers understand. The result? A **near-native experience** with minimal input lag, proving that WebAssembly isn’t just for modern games—it’s a **bridge to the past**.
