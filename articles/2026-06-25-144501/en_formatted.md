# Don't Rely on Secs-man, a Secrets Manager for the Doomed

Meet Secs-man, a secrets manager designed to be abandoned, but still useful for storing encrypted local backups of secrets. Can you trust it?

## 🔑 The Core of This Topic
Secs-man is a secrets manager built with the intention of being abandoned. Its creators want it to be usable even if the software disappears from the face of the Earth. This raises questions about trust and reliance on software.

## ⚡ 5-Second Key Points
* **Point 1**: Secs-man stores encrypted local backups of secrets.
* **Point 2**: It's designed to be abandoned, but still useful.
* **Point 3**: This challenges traditional notions of trust in software.

## 📈 Detailed Breakdown
**Local Encrypted Backups**: Secs-man stores encrypted backups of secrets locally on your device. This means you have control over your data, even if the software disappears.

**Abandonware**: The creators of Secs-man want it to be abandoned. They aim to make it usable even if the software is no longer maintained or supported.

> 💡 Insight: By designing a secrets manager to be abandoned, Secs-man challenges the traditional notion that software should be reliable and trustworthy.

## 🎯 Real-World Impact
* Storing sensitive information locally can reduce dependence on cloud services.
* Encrypted backups provide an additional layer of security.
* This approach may inspire new designs for software that prioritize user autonomy.

## ✨ Conclusion
Secs-man is a thought-provoking project that raises important questions about trust and reliance on software. While it may not be the most conventional secrets manager, its design offers a unique perspective on the importance of user autonomy and control over sensitive information.
