## 🔑 The Core of This Topic
An amateur linguist, Liam T. Burnett, has proposed a novel approach to deciphering Linear A, the undeciphered script of the Minoan civilization. His method, detailed online, suggests a phonetic interpretation of the script, moving beyond previous attempts that focused on syllabic or logographic readings. This could finally unlock the language of a major Bronze Age European culture.

## 📈 Detailed Breakdown
**The Script's History**
Linear A was used by the Minoan civilization on Crete from the 18th to the 15th century BCE. Unlike its successor, Linear B, which was deciphered as an early form of Greek, Linear A has resisted all attempts, leaving its language and purpose unknown.

**Burnett's Hypothesis**
Burnett's work suggests that Linear A might be decipherable using a phonetic approach, where symbols represent individual sounds rather than syllables or entire words. He has identified potential phonetic values for many signs, drawing parallels with other ancient scripts and linguistic patterns.

> 💡 Insight: The breakthrough hinges on treating Linear A signs as representing sounds, a departure from many previous, unsuccessful decipherment efforts.

**Supporting Evidence**
While still under scrutiny by the academic community, Burnett's proposal includes comparisons of Linear A texts with known linguistic structures and the context of Minoan artifacts. Early analyses suggest potential word formations and grammatical patterns consistent with his phonetic interpretation.
