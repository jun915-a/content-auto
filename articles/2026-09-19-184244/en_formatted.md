What if the very chaos of random signals—Johnson noise—could transmit data? A groundbreaking study reveals how modulated noise might revolutionize secure, low-power communication, bridging gaps in quantum and classical systems alike.

## 🔑 The Core of This Topic

Johnson noise—a ubiquitous thermal fluctuation—has long been dismissed as mere electronic interference. Yet, this study demonstrates its potential as a **carrier of information**, proving that by carefully modulating this noise, data can be transmitted reliably, even under extreme conditions. The breakthrough lies in exploiting noise’s inherent randomness as a structured signal, enabling communication where traditional methods fail.
