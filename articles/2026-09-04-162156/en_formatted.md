# Google Drops Custom Domain 'Send As' in 2027: What You Need to Know

Google is discontinuing the ability to use custom domains for 'Send As' in Gmail by January 2027. This change impacts businesses and organizations relying on branded email aliases. Learn how it affects you and what alternatives exist to maintain seamless communication.

## 🔑 The Core of This Topic
Google is phasing out the **‘Send As’** feature for custom domains in Gmail starting January 2027. This means users will no longer be able to send emails from a custom domain alias (e.g., `john@company.com` instead of `john+alias@company.com`), forcing a shift toward alternative solutions for branded email identities.

## ⚡ 5-Second Key Points
- **No more ‘Send As’ aliases**: Custom domain aliases (e.g., `support@company.com`) will stop working.
- **January 2027 deadline**: The change is scheduled for early next year—no extensions.
- **Impact on businesses**: Branded email identities and workflows may require reconfiguration.
- **Alternatives exist**: Google Workspace’s **‘Reply-To’** and **‘From’** address customization may fill the gap.
- **Migration required**: Organizations must plan updates to avoid disruptions.

## 📈 Detailed Breakdown
**Element 1: What ‘Send As’ Does (and Why It’s Disappearing)**
The **‘Send As’** feature allowed users to send emails from a custom domain alias (e.g., `john@company.com` instead of their personal Gmail address). This was useful for teams sharing a single domain account or for personalizing outbound emails. Google is retiring this due to **security and usability concerns**, particularly around phishing risks tied to alias-based sending. The shift aligns with broader industry trends toward stricter email authentication (like DMARC).

**Element 2: What Replaces ‘Send As’?**
Google Workspace users can still **customize the ‘From’ and ‘Reply-To’ addresses** in emails, but these won’t include full alias functionality. For example:
- You can set a **default ‘From’ address** (e.g., `team@company.com`) for all outgoing emails.
- The **‘Reply-To’** field can be adjusted to direct responses to a specific address.

> 💡 Insight: **This isn’t a full replacement**, but it preserves some branding control. Businesses will need to rely on **Google Workspace’s ‘External Sharing’ settings** or third-party tools for advanced aliasing.

## 🎯 Real-World Impact
- **Businesses relying on shared inboxes** (e.g., `support@`, `sales@`) may need to reassign or consolidate accounts.
- **Marketing teams** using alias-based campaigns (e.g., `newsletter@`) will lose direct control over sender identities.
- **Security teams** may face challenges as alias-based phishing attempts become harder to track.
- **Customers may notice changes** in reply addresses, potentially affecting trust in automated responses.

## ✨ Conclusion
Google’s discontinuation of **‘Send As’ for custom domains** marks a significant shift in how businesses manage email identities. While alternatives like **‘From’ address customization** provide partial solutions, organizations should **audit their email workflows now** and explore tools like **Google Workspace’s ‘External Sharing’** or third-party solutions (e.g., **Zapier, Mailgun**) to maintain seamless communication. The deadline is **January 2027—don’t wait until the last minute.**
