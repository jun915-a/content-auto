# Why Your Monitor Slowed Down Your Downloads by 75%

Ever wondered why your downloads feel sluggish even with a high-speed connection? Your monitor might be silently siphoning bandwidth. Discover how Ethernet over DisplayPort (DoP) works, why it drains your speeds, and how to fix it—without sacrificing display quality.

## 🔑 The Core of This Topic
Your monitor could be **stealing bandwidth** by using Ethernet over DisplayPort (DoP) to transmit data, which diverts precious network resources away from your downloads. This happens when your monitor acts as a network bridge, splitting your connection between display and data—often reducing speeds by **up to 75%**. The issue stems from how DoP shares bandwidth between video and network traffic, leaving less for your primary tasks like downloads or gaming.

## ⚡ 5-Second Key Points
- **DoP (DisplayPort over Ethernet) consumes bandwidth** by merging video and network data, slowing down non-display traffic.
- **Most monitors prioritize display quality**, leaving little bandwidth for downloads or online activities.
- **Disabling DoP or using a dedicated Ethernet adapter** can restore full download speeds without sacrificing visuals.

## 📈 Detailed Breakdown
**The Bandwidth Thief: How DoP Works**
DisplayPort over Ethernet (DoP) is designed to **combine video and network traffic** into a single cable, reducing clutter. However, this comes with a trade-off: the monitor **allocates a portion of your bandwidth** to transmitting video signals, leaving less for other uses like downloads or online gaming. If your monitor supports DoP, it may be **automatically enabled**, silently draining your connection without your knowledge. This is especially problematic for high-bandwidth tasks where every megabit counts.

**Why Your Downloads Suffer**
When DoP is active, your monitor acts as a **network bridge**, splitting your available bandwidth between two functions: video transmission and data transfer. If your display requires significant bandwidth (e.g., 4K resolution or high refresh rates), the remaining data throughput for downloads can drop dramatically—sometimes by **70-80%**. This isn’t just a minor inconvenience; it can turn a 100 Mbps connection into a frustratingly slow 25 Mbps experience.

> 💡 Insight: **Not all monitors support DoP**—check your model’s specs. If it doesn’t, you’re safe, but if it does, you may need to manually disable it or use a separate Ethernet adapter to avoid the bottleneck.

## 🎯 Real-World Impact
- **Slower downloads**: Streaming movies or updating software takes **twice as long** as expected, even with a fast ISP.
- **Gaming lag**: Online multiplayer games suffer from **higher ping and packet loss** due to competing bandwidth demands.
- **Unexpected buffering**: Video calls or cloud backups **freeze or stall** when the monitor is actively using DoP.

## ✨ Conclusion
Your monitor might be the **unexpected villain** in your slow download saga. By understanding how DoP works—and whether your setup is affected—you can take simple steps to **restore full bandwidth** without compromising your display. Whether it’s disabling DoP in your monitor’s settings or investing in a dedicated Ethernet adapter, small changes can lead to **dramatic speed improvements**. Don’t let your monitor hold you back—optimize your setup today!
