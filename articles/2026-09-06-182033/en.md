# Kadō: Open-Source Habit Tracker Redefines Progress Tracking for iOS

Meet **Kadō**, an open-source habit tracker breaking traditional scoring with a **non-binary system**—perfect for iOS users tired of rigid checklists. Lightweight, privacy-focused, and designed for nuanced self-tracking, it’s a breath of fresh air for habit enthusiasts. Dive into its unique features and why it stands out in a crowded space.

## 🔑 The Core of This Topic
Kadō is an **open-source habit tracker** for iOS that flips the script on traditional habit apps. Instead of forcing users into binary 'done/not done' tracking, it embraces a **non-binary scoring system**, allowing for granular, self-reflective progress. Built with privacy and simplicity in mind, Kadō caters to those who want to track habits without the pressure of rigid metrics or bloated interfaces. It’s a minimalist’s dream for anyone who values **flexibility, transparency, and personal growth** over algorithmic perfection.

## ⚡ 5-Second Key Points
- **Non-binary scoring**: Track habits on a **spectrum** (e.g., 'mostly did it') instead of just checked/unchecked.
- **Open-source & privacy-first**: No ads, no data collection—just you and your habits.
- **iOS-native design**: Clean, intuitive interface tailored for Apple devices.
- **Lightweight & free**: No subscriptions, no gimmicks—just pure functionality.
- **Customizable & flexible**: Add habits, set reminders, and visualize trends without constraints.

## 📈 Detailed Breakdown
**A Radical Shift from Binary Tracking**
Most habit apps force users into a **black-and-white world**—either you did it or you didn’t. Kadō smashes this paradigm by introducing a **sliding scale** for scoring. Want to acknowledge that you *mostly* meditated but got distracted? Or that you *partially* stuck to your workout routine? Kadō lets you **self-assess** without guilt or false perfectionism. This approach aligns with modern psychology’s emphasis on **progress over perfection**, making it ideal for those who struggle with all-or-nothing thinking.
