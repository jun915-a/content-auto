# Snail Teeth: Nature's New Strongest Material Unseated Spider Silk

Forget spider silk! Researchers have discovered that the teeth of a humble sea snail are stronger. This slimy marvel could revolutionize material science.

## 🔑 The Core of This Topic
The proboscis of the *Patella vulgata* limpet, a common sea snail, possesses teeth made of a unique material that exhibits incredible strength and toughness, surpassing even the renowned strength of spider silk. This discovery challenges long-held assumptions about natural materials.

## ⚡ 5-Second Key Points
- **Snail Teeth Triumph**: Limpet teeth are now considered nature's strongest material.
- **Exceptional Strength**: They are tougher and stronger than spider silk.
- **Biomimicry Potential**: Could inspire new super-strong, lightweight materials.

## 📈 Detailed Breakdown
**Limpet Teeth Structure**
The teeth are made of goethite, an iron hydroxide mineral. Unlike typical biological materials, these teeth have a fibrous structure that prevents crack propagation, making them exceptionally resilient and resistant to fracture under stress.

**Comparison to Spider Silk**
While spider silk is known for its high tensile strength, limpet teeth possess superior toughness. Toughness measures a material's ability to absorb energy and deform without fracturing. This makes the snail's teeth more durable in real-world conditions.

> 💡 Insight: The combination of mineral composition and intricate fibrous structure in limpet teeth creates a material far superior to previously known natural substances.
