# Scarf Leaves Haskell After 7 Years

After seven years in production, Scarf has reluctantly moved away from Haskell.

## 🔑 The Core of This Topic
Scarf, a seven-year-old project, has finally parted ways with Haskell. This move comes as a surprise to many, given the language's reputation for reliability and performance. However, the team behind Scarf has been vocal about their dissatisfaction with Haskell's limitations.

## ⚡ 5-Second Key Points
* **Point 1**: Performance issues hindered the project's growth.
* **Point 2**: Haskell's ecosystem proved restrictive for Scarf's needs.
* **Point 3**: The team sought a more flexible and scalable solution.

## 📈 Detailed Breakdown
**Element 1**
Scarf's reliance on Haskell led to performance issues, which in turn affected the project's growth. As the team expanded, so did the complexity of their codebase. However, Haskell's strict type system and lack of flexibility made it difficult for the team to adapt to these changes.

**Element 2**
Haskell's ecosystem, while extensive, proved restrictive for Scarf's needs. The team encountered limitations in libraries and frameworks, which hindered their ability to innovate and push the boundaries of what was possible. This led to a sense of stagnation and a desire for a more open-ended solution.

> 💡 Insight: The move away from Haskell highlights the importance of choosing a language that aligns with the project's needs and growth trajectory.

## 🎯 Real-World Impact
* Improved performance and scalability
* Enhanced flexibility and adaptability
* Better alignment with project goals and vision

## ✨ Conclusion
Scarf's departure from Haskell serves as a reminder that even the most well-established projects can benefit from change. By acknowledging limitations and seeking new solutions, teams can ensure their projects remain scalable, flexible, and aligned with their goals.
