# Quad9 Exits France: Why DNS Blocking Fails to Combat Piracy

Privacy-focused DNS resolver Quad9 has withdrawn from France following a court order demanding it block piracy sites. This move underscores the problematic trend of forcing internet infrastructure providers into content policing, highlighting the ineffectiveness of DNS blocking and its dangerous implications for internet freedom.

## 🔑 The Core of This Topic
Quad9, a major non-profit DNS resolver known for its privacy and security features, has ceased operations in France. This decision stems from a French court order compelling it to block access to certain piracy websites. The core issue is the misguided attempt to combat piracy by targeting DNS resolvers, which are foundational internet services, rather than the sources of illegal content. This approach is ineffective and sets a troubling precedent for internet censorship.

## ⚡ 5-Second Key Points
- **Quad9 Withdraws**: Privacy DNS resolver Quad9 exits France due to a piracy-related blocking order.
- **DNS Blocking Ineffective**: Targeting DNS resolvers is a futile attempt to stop piracy, easily bypassed by users.
- **Dangerous Precedent**: This action forces infrastructure providers into content policing, threatening internet freedom.

## 📈 Detailed Breakdown
**Element 1: The French Court Order's Reach**
The French court ordered Quad9 to block specific domains associated with piracy. This is particularly concerning because Quad9 is a non-profit organization that doesn't host content or profit from it, yet it's being held responsible for content accessible via its service. This broad legal reach burdens neutral internet infrastructure.

**Element 2: The Futility of DNS Blocking**
DNS blocking is an easily circumvented measure. Users can simply switch to another DNS resolver, use a VPN, or access content directly via IP addresses. This means the court order is unlikely to significantly impact piracy but will inconvenience legitimate users and push sophisticated users to less secure alternatives.

> 💡 Insight: Forcing DNS resolvers to block content is akin to asking a phone book publisher to police illegal conversations; it addresses the symptom, not the source, and disproportionately impacts neutral intermediaries.

## 🎯 Real-World Impact
- **Increased Censorship Risk**: Sets a precedent where governments can compel DNS providers to block content, potentially extending beyond piracy to political or social speech.
- **Erosion of Internet Freedom**: Undermines the principle of a free and open internet by turning critical infrastructure into enforcement points.
- **Disrupted Services**: Legitimate users in France lose access to Quad9's privacy and security benefits, potentially forcing them onto less secure DNS services.

## ✨ Conclusion
Quad9's departure from France highlights the critical flaw in using DNS resolvers as a tool to fight piracy. This approach is not only ineffective but also dangerous, setting a precedent that threatens internet freedom and privacy globally. More effective solutions should target content hosts and distributors, not the neutral infrastructure that underpins the internet.
