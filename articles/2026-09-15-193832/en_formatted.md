# Transforming a $20 Hotspot into a DIY Texting Device

A clever hack turns a budget 4G hotspot into a functional texting device, bypassing traditional carriers. Explore the build, its quirks, and why it matters for off-grid communication.

## 🔑 The Core of This Topic
A **$20 4G wireless hotspot**—typically used for internet access—has been repurposed into a **standalone texting device** by repackaging its SIM card and firmware. This project strips away carrier restrictions, allowing users to send/receive SMS without relying on a smartphone or carrier plan. The hack leverages open-source tools and clever software workarounds to emulate a traditional messaging device.

## ⚡ 5-Second Key Points
- **No carrier dependency**: Uses a SIM card directly with custom firmware.
- **Offline-capable**: Works independently of smartphones or internet.
- **Budget-friendly**: Costs less than $20 with existing hardware.

## 📈 Detailed Breakdown
**Element 1**
The project begins with a **4G LTE hotspot** (e.g., a TP-Link or similar device) that’s repurposed by flashing **OpenWRT firmware**. This replaces the manufacturer’s locked-down software with a flexible Linux environment. The key step is configuring the device to **route SMS traffic** through its built-in modem, bypassing the carrier’s gatekeeping. Users can then send/receive texts via a **command-line interface (CLI)** or a simple web interface—no smartphone required.

**Element 2**
The hack’s brilliance lies in its **simplicity for basic needs**. While it lacks features like rich media or group chats, it excels at **point-to-point messaging**, making it ideal for remote work, emergency communication, or areas with unreliable carrier infrastructure. The project’s GitHub page ([modem-thing](https://bkovac.github.io/modem-thing/)) includes step-by-step guides, but caution is advised: **bricking the device is possible** if steps aren’t followed precisely.

> 💡 Insight: This project proves that **repurposing consumer hardware** can unlock unexpected functionality—here, turning a hotspot into a **texting relic of the pre-smartphone era**.

## 📈 Real-World Impact
- **Emergency communication**: Reliable SMS in disaster zones where networks collapse.
- **Off-grid work**: Remote workers in rural areas avoid carrier blackouts.
- **DIY tech education**: Encourages tinkering with open-source firmware and hardware.

## ✨ Conclusion
This hack is a **proof of concept**—not a polished product, but a creative solution for those who need **low-cost, carrier-independent messaging**. While it won’t replace modern apps, it’s a fascinating example of how **constraints breed innovation**. Perfect for makers, hackers, or anyone tired of carrier monopolies.
